# Dream Valley Poem Poster Structure

## Reverse-engineered source DNA

- Large surreal landscape with a dominant field of flowers, grass, sky, or cloud.
- One or two tiny human figures provide scale.
- The view often feels impossible: steep slope, floating valley, layered sky, or tunnel-like opening.
- Poetry or text sits lightly in the space rather than dominating it.
- The palette is vivid but not neon: green, orange, pink, blue, cream, white.
- Mood is spacious, dreamlike, and quietly epic.

## Layout families

- **wide-horizon-valley:** broad sky over rolling flower fields and a low text zone.
- **center-path-garden:** a path or opening leads into the scene and carries text.
- **surreal-vertical-valley:** steep valley walls or layered slopes with a central text anchor.
- **flower-sea-portal:** flowers open into a sky portal, architectural skyline, or cloud chamber.
- **tiny-figure-scale:** one small figure or pair places the poem inside a huge dream world.

## Text placement rules

- Keep the poem short.
- Place the text in a clear zone: sky, path, open slope, or cloud gap.
- Let the text be small against the landscape unless one anchor word needs to be larger.
- Avoid full-page poem blocks unless the user explicitly wants editorial text density.

## Scale and surrealism rules

- Tiny people, lone walkers, distant figures, or a small building edge can stabilize the dream scale.
- Surreal perspective should feel airy and natural, not fractured into collage pieces.
- Use repetition only if it reinforces the dream field, such as repeated flower patches or cloud bands.

## Color and texture rules

- Dominant tones: green meadow, orange flower, pink blossom, blue sky, white cloud, cream haze.
- Keep colors saturated but soft-edged and printed, not neon.
- Add painterly blur, diffusion, or light grain to the scene if needed.
- Keep the whole image readable as a poster rather than a pure illustration spread.

## Prompt recipe

1. Canvas and landscape field: ratio, openness, horizon or slope.
2. Scene structure: flowers, clouds, path, figure, or architecture.
3. Poem placement: short lines, location, scale, anchor word.
4. Color and atmosphere: palette, blur, distance, surreal print feel.
5. Mood and avoids: dreamlike tone plus hard constraints.

## Hard avoids

- Full-bleed realistic landscape photo
- Dense scrapbook or collage clutter
- Commercial poster hierarchy
- Clean UI or infographic layout
- Heavy calligraphy-only composition
- Neon cyberpunk fantasy
- Too many competing landmarks

## Example prompt skeleton

```text
Tall poster or wide dreamscape with a vast floral valley and open sky, printed with soft grain and gentle surreal distance.

Place one or two tiny figures in the field to give scale, and keep the scene airy with flower slopes, cloud masses, or a portal-like opening.

Set the poem lightly in the open space, small and clear, with one anchor word allowed to be slightly larger if it helps the composition.

Use vivid green, orange, pink, and blue tones with printed softness; avoid busy collage, commercial typography, neon, and full-bleed realism.
```
