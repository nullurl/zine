# Style Grammar

## Reference Deconstruction

The references form a numbered literary-poster series. Each page uses a warm cream paper field and a stable editorial scaffold, then changes one accent color and one lower collage image.

### Page Scaffold

- tall 3:5 or 2:3 portrait page
- warm ivory paper with subtle mottling, fibers, dust, and edge wear
- upper-left small corner rule, colored index square, and tiny uppercase category label
- upper-right tiny edition or metadata line
- author name in large dark high-contrast serif
- italic outline title/keyword overlapping the author or quote area
- large quote in literary italic serif, aligned left with generous line height
- one final clause emphasized in the accent color
- lower-right or lower-center torn-paper image/material anchor
- small footer label, rule, page number, and corner bracket

### Type Hierarchy

The author and quote are not equal weights:

1. author/display name
2. outlined title or keyword
3. quote block
4. highlighted final clause
5. attribution and archive microtype

Use a maximum of three type families: display serif, italic quote serif, and narrow microtype.

### Image Grammar

The lower anchor is usually one of:

- faded photograph of architecture, desert, tree, road, room, or landscape
- torn monochrome print with one colored paper or brush shape
- translucent fabric, leaf, branch, egg, bird, sun, or window study
- geometric diagram, orbit, dot field, or halftone grid behind a central object

The image does not explain the quote literally. It acts as a quiet counter-image or material echo.

## Minimal Zine Fusion

Retain:

- large paper field and negative space
- one clear visual anchor
- one restrained saturated color
- small archive details
- old print defects and flat scanned-paper capture
- distant, poetic, editorial temperature

Adapt:

- the tiny object cluster becomes a lower-page torn collage
- the tiny caption becomes a full quote hierarchy
- the single color anchor is repeated sparingly across index, quote emphasis, outline word, and image detail
- the emotional object becomes a literature/image relationship

## Accent Behavior

The reference system rotates one accent per page:

- rust red: autumn, death, arrival, memory, old architecture
- cobalt blue: bird, birth, sky, philosophical rupture
- burnt orange: desert, heat, survival, summer
- violet: solitude, room, interior life
- mustard: age, desire, golden memory
- leaf green: aloneness, field, growth

Do not combine the whole series palette on one page.

## Failure Diagnosis

### Looks like a social quote template

Remove centered text, rounded cards, motivational hierarchy, bright gradients, and generic icons. Restore left editorial alignment, oversized serif name, outline keyword, paper fibers, and a lower torn anchor.

### Looks like a book cover

Reduce author branding and title dominance. Add quote line hierarchy, footer metadata, and an image that behaves like an archive fragment rather than a cover illustration.

### Looks like a collage with text added afterward

Make text and image share one axis: let the outline keyword cross the image edge, let the quote rule align with the collage, or let the accent appear in both quote and material anchor.

### Text is wrong

Shorten the quote, state exact line breaks, use only user-supplied copy, or leave a clean reserved area for later typesetting.
