## 1. Stable Identity

The reference set is a family of clean botanical cutout posters. Each image uses one large flat color field, one off-white instant-photo frame, one natural plant photograph inside the frame, and a branch or flower group extending outside the frame. The visual effect is graphic and dimensional without becoming a 3D mockup.

Stable signals:

- tall portrait canvas
- solid pink, green, blue, sage, pine, periwinkle, or mustard field
- single white rectangular frame, slightly offset from center
- generous blank lower frame strip
- realistic plant photographed against a simple field or soft botanical background
- foreground branch crossing the white border
- clean cutout contour with natural petal translucency
- no text or only negligible caption text
- flat studio light and quiet paper surface

## 2. Composition Measurements

Typical canvas:

- outer field: 65%-88% of the page
- white frame: 45%-72% canvas width and 52%-78% canvas height
- frame offset: 2%-12% of frame width or height
- white border: 3%-7% of frame width
- photo window: 76%-90% frame width and 65%-82% frame height
- blank lower caption strip: 10%-18% frame height
- plant overflow: 12%-35% of canvas

The frame generally sits near center or slightly upper-right. The plant exits lower-left, lower-right, upper-center, or along a diagonal. The exterior branch should not become a second unrelated image; it is a continuation of the interior specimen.

## 3. Overflow Geometry

### Blossom overflow

A flowering branch fills the inner window, then one large cluster crosses the lower or side border. The exterior blossoms can be closer and sharper than the framed photo, but the branch direction must remain continuous.

### Single stem rise

One vertical or diagonal stem starts outside the frame, passes through the photo window, and exits through the top. Use for plum blossom, magnolia, lotus stem, fern, or seed head.

### Corner escape

The photo window remains legible while a flower group expands beyond one corner into the color field. Keep the empty opposite corner quiet.

### Counterbranch

The interior image shows a soft plant context and the exterior cutout supplies a sharper foreground branch from the opposite direction. Both must share the same light and species.

## 4. Color Roles

| Role | Typical colors | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| outer field | dusty rose, forest green, sky blue, sage, pine, mustard | flat, uninterrupted, high coverage |
| frame | warm white, paper white | neutral contrast anchor |
| photo window | related but slightly varied field/plant environment | softer, photographic, lower edge contrast |
| botanical subject | natural pink, white, magenta, green, brown, yellow | most detailed and recognizable |
| stem and shadow | charcoal brown, deep green, muted black | structural counterweight |
| tiny accent | flower center, leaf tip, or paper grain | below 2% unless part of plant |

Reference field families:

- **Blush blossom:** dusty rose field, pink flowers, brown stem, white frame.
- **Pine lotus:** deep green field, magenta/purple flower, green leaves, white frame.
- **Sky branch:** medium blue field, white blossom, dark branch, white frame.
- **Sage bloom:** muted sage field, white/pale pink blossom, green foliage, white frame.
- **Pine magnolia:** deep pine field, white flowers, dark branches, white frame.
- **Mustard tree:** mustard field, dark tree silhouette, blue-gray water or sky photo, white frame.

When preserving a reference palette, lock the field hue first. Then maintain the frame neutral and keep the botanical subject naturally separated from the field. Never use a global gradient to simulate color richness.

## 5. Botanical and Frame Treatment

- The inner photo has slight photographic softness or natural depth, but stays flat within the frame.
- The exterior plant has clean cutout edges, subtle translucency, and a soft contact shadow where it crosses the frame.
- The frame should be matte, slightly textured, and evenly white; avoid thick ornamental vintage borders.
- The blank caption strip is an intentional quiet zone, not a UI form field.
- A branch may cover the frame border but should not cover the whole caption strip unless the composition is explicitly overflow-heavy.
- Keep one species and one light direction. The branch should not change scale or anatomy at the boundary.

## 6. Minimal Zine Fusion

Preserve:

- vertical paper canvas
- one visual anchor
- large negative space
- restrained or absent typography
- flat scanned surface
- one coherent emotional temperature

Adapt:

- the small Minimal Zine object becomes a large framed botanical photograph
- the single color anchor becomes a full color field when the reference demands it
- the photo/cutout boundary becomes the main conceptual device
- paper border replaces scattered collage fragments

## 7. Exclusions and Diagnostics

- **Looks like a Polaroid stack:** remove all extra frames, cards, tapes, captions, stickers, and photos; keep one frame only.
- **Plant stays inside:** explicitly state `the same continuous branch exits the white border and overlaps the outer color field in front of the frame`.
- **Plant is floating:** add one contact shadow at the frame crossing and align branch direction, light, and scale across the boundary.
- **Field becomes a gradient:** repeat `one flat solid printed color, no gradient, no room, no lighting falloff`.
- **Color drifts:** state the background hex family and color roles; prohibit global tint and palette mixing.
- **Looks like a flower advertisement:** remove brand typography, product language, vases, packaging, and glossy studio campaign lighting.
- **Too empty:** enlarge the overflow branch or extend it across a second frame edge, but do not add unrelated props.
- **Too busy:** remove the second branch, secondary flowers, and all decorative symbols before reducing frame size.
