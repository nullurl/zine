# Aperture System

## Shape Construction

Build one opening from three layers:

1. **Primary contour:** arch, taper, flame, doorway, or tear.
2. **Edge material:** branches, leaves, soot, rock, fibers, chipped ink, or emulsion.
3. **Detached echoes:** 3-12 small flecks or holes that lead the eye toward the primary opening.

The primary contour must remain readable at thumbnail size. Detached echoes cannot form a second competing scene.

## Aperture Families

### Canopy Window

- dark leaves create the side walls
- thin branches cross only the upper 10%-25% of the opening
- lower edge stays wide enough for a horizon and figures
- suitable for home, family, summer, water, and memory

### Cave Mouth

- rock or deep interior creates a strong dark foreground
- avoid realistic horror-cave detail
- use a distant bright landscape and simple ground plane
- suitable for passage, emergence, exile, and return

### Burned Paper

- edge shows fibers, soot, char, and irregular curling
- no glowing fire border unless explicitly requested
- scene below looks like another printed layer
- suitable for loss, recovery, afterimage, and archive

### Eroded Ink

- black screenprint chips away with crisp broken edges
- small flecks share the same ink logic
- avoid photographic dust overlays
- suitable for fading memory, time, identity, and absence

### Flame Void

- tall central opening narrows upward
- shape suggests flame without depicting literal fire
- interior horizon remains horizontal and stable
- suitable for hope, devotion, signal, and resilience

### Hidden Door

- geometric doorway or narrow rectangle is partially overtaken by organic darkness
- keep one straight edge visible so structure survives
- suitable for decision, home, future, and transition

### Emulsion Tear

- photographic or printed surface peels into a warm under-image
- edge combines thin film, scratches, and torn fibers
- suitable for film memory, dream, time, and recovered place

## Placement Rules

- center axis or 3%-8% off-center
- lower-middle by default
- opening top reaches no higher than the upper third unless it remains narrow
- widest part sits near the horizon or foreground relation
- tiny title stays outside the opening, separated by substantial black space

## Scene Budget

Use no more than:

- one celestial sign
- one distant landform
- one middle-ground surface
- one foreground relationship
- one tiny title

Remove elements in this order when crowded: title, celestial sign, secondary figure, extra terrain. Preserve the aperture and one relationship.
