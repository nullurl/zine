# Vellum Editorial Zine Style Grammar

Use this reference to convert supplied visuals into a safe, reusable prompt grammar. Abstract the design logic; do not copy names, logos, menu language, or exact compositions.

## Visual Thesis

The page is a quiet editorial object photographed from above: a useful image is held between opaque paper and tracing paper. Transparency creates hierarchy, not decoration. The viewer should sense a portfolio cover, studio dossier, menu insert, or personal image sheet assembled by hand.

## Canvas and Surface

- Default to a portrait 2:3 canvas, with 3:4 acceptable for a softer paper-card proportion.
- Use a tabletop or board surface behind the sheet: cool gray, foggy olive, deep forest green, warm taupe, soft brown, or blue gray.
- Avoid polished stone, glossy acrylic, and dramatic studio reflections.
- Keep the surrounding surface visible as a quiet frame around the paper.
- Use diffuse overhead light, low-to-medium contrast, and shallow contact shadows.

## Layer Geometry

The stable stack is:

1. colored or neutral background surface
2. white or cream backing sheet, slightly rotated
3. one framed photograph or image field
4. translucent tracing-paper sheet offset across the image
5. small title and information block
6. one attachment or index detail

The backing sheet often begins near the upper-left and the vellum extends lower or rightward. The offset should be intentional enough to show a paper edge. Transparency should veil the image without erasing it.

## Attachment Cue

A metal paper clip near the upper-left corner is a strong physical signal. It anchors the stack and makes the page feel handled rather than rendered. Keep it thin, simple, and slightly reflective. Do not add multiple clips, binders, staples, tape, or office supplies unless the recipe explicitly asks for them.

## Image Grammar

Use one main rectangular image:

- portrait or human gesture
- quiet interior or architecture
- café table or food detail
- textile or material surface
- abstract dark field
- object or process photograph
- landscape fragment

Give the image a clean white or cream margin. A second smaller image is allowed only when it belongs to the same visual story. Do not turn the page into a grid.

The image may be partially hidden by vellum, but its focal feature should remain identifiable. Avoid over-sharpening and preserve gentle film or print softness.

## Vellum Behavior

The overlay is:

- pale translucent gray, white, mint, or warm ivory
- matte and fibrous rather than plastic
- slightly larger than the title block
- offset or lightly rotated, never perfectly aligned with every sheet
- opaque enough to carry type, transparent enough to reveal image structure

Use one overlay for the default. Two overlays can create a deliberate double exposure, but each must have a distinct purpose.

## Typography

Use a compact editorial hierarchy:

- one title, 2-5 words
- one small uppercase or spaced subline
- one short descriptor or two tiny lines
- optional date, location, or edition index

Type may be centered on vellum, aligned to a quiet edge, or placed near the lower third. The title should not become a poster headline. Use serif, narrow sans, restrained grotesk, monospaced, or one subtle script accent depending on the recipe.

Invent neutral short text when the user gives no wording. Replace visible reference branding with thematic copy such as a place, material, season, or concept. Never imitate a real brand identity.

## Color Grammar

The base colors are desaturated:

- gray paper and gray surface
- cream and faded white
- foggy sage or olive
- deep green or brown
- graphite or softened black

Choose one accent:

- oxblood or burgundy label
- deep green field
- faded cobalt mark
- black title against a pale sheet
- warm ivory type on a dark background

Keep the accent to 1%-6% of the canvas. Transparency itself is not a color accent. A second strong hue usually makes the page look like a campaign template.

## Physical Reproduction

Use:

- paper fibers
- faint mottling
- subtle offset print
- soft halftone or photocopy texture
- edge wear
- very shallow paper shadows
- slight differences in opacity

Avoid:

- bevels
- glossy plastic
- glass glare
- heavy ambient occlusion
- cinematic spotlights
- floating 3D sheets

## Anti-Identity

Reject anything that reads as:

- luxury branding mockup
- corporate brochure cover
- UI card interface
- full-bleed photography poster
- wedding advertisement
- stock agency portfolio
- menu template with dense copy
- fake social media graphic
- generic stationery flat lay

The page should feel authored and quiet, not commercially optimized.

## Reference Extraction Checklist

Record:

1. surface color and material
2. backing-sheet tone and rotation
3. image count and image role
4. vellum count, tint, and offset
5. clip or attachment cue
6. title alignment and type voice
7. supporting-copy amount
8. accent color and area
9. shadow and grain behavior
10. three anti-identity constraints

Then translate those observations into visible prompt clauses. Never include source paths, copied names, or analysis terms in the final prompt.
