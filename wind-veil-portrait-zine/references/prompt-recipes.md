# Prompt Recipes

Use these recipes when translating a theme into the wind-veil portrait system.

## Closeup Sweep

Use for intimate, emotional, or forceful portrait briefs.

- one close subject with hair blown across the face
- white or pale cloth fills one side of the frame
- red ribbon or cloth streaks cut through the foreground

## Veil Crossing

Use when the user wants concealment, softness, or mystery.

- translucent fabric crosses the face
- the eye and cheek stay readable through the veil
- background stays soft and abstract

## Ribbon Orbit

Use when the motion should feel energetic and graphic.

- red ribbon or fabric loops around the subject in arcs
- motion lines or particles reinforce the orbit
- keep the subject calm against the movement

## Backlit Fragment

Use for luminous, cinematic, or heroic portraits.

- backlight traces hair and cloth edges
- face is softly modelled, not overexposed
- background falls away into blur and atmosphere

## Edge-Cut Portrait

Use when the image needs tension or asymmetry.

- crop the subject against one side or corner
- let fabric and ribbon exit the frame
- use empty blur or sky to balance the composition

## Particle Storm

Use for wind, rain, petals, dust, or energetic ambience.

- floating particles fill the near field
- the subject remains the anchor
- blur stays photographic, not painterly

## Armored Vanguard

Use for warriors, generals, guardians, or battle-memory portraits.

- close subject in layered armor or heavy battle cloth
- one weapon shaft, spear, or sword hilt gives the frame a strong diagonal
- metal trims catch warm highlights while the face stays the focal point

## Mask And Ember

Use for ferocity, secrecy, ritual combat, or wounded resolve.

- mouth guard, talisman strip, scarf, or partial mask hides the lower face
- ember sparks or ash marks float through the foreground
- keep one eye or brow fully readable

## Ruin Procession

Use when the background needs scale and story without becoming a full scene.

- broken walls, scaffold shapes, city ruins, or temple fragments stay soft and distant
- banners and fabric lead the eye through the environment
- the subject still dominates the frame

## Wuxia Key Visual

Use for Chinese ancient landscape posters, heroine/hero key art, and scene-rich wuxia briefs.

- close subject in the foreground, but the landscape spans the full width
- add mountain gates, bamboo, tiled eaves, pavilions, courtyards, rivers, mist, and banners
- keep one red, gold, or vermilion accent as the motion anchor

## Mountain Gate Scene

Use when the composition should feel like an entrance, duel, or journey.

- mountain gate, temple arch, or fortress pass frames the figure
- cloth, hair, and banners sweep through the opening
- distant peaks or walls stay soft but clearly Chinese in character

## Glow Siege

Use for dense battlefield light, embers, and abstract crowd energy.

- one close anchor face, with crowd haze dissolving behind it
- gold, teal, and ember bokeh fill the frame without becoming neon
- reflective armor and weapon metal catch fragmented highlights

## Blade Screen

Use when the sword or staff should structure the image.

- a blade, spear, or staff cuts the foreground diagonally
- the face is partially revealed behind the weapon plane
- sparks or cloth fragments trace along the blade edge

## Iridescent Vanguard

Use for glossy armor, wet lacquer, and dark reflective materials.

- black lacquer, bronze trim, teal reflections, and molten gold highlights
- armor reads as heavy and real, not plastic
- the body rotates through the light so edges flash and fall back into shadow

## Prompt Skeleton

```text
Portrait 4:5 cinematic wind-swept poster, matte photographic finish, shallow depth of field, soft backlight, one close human subject with large flowing white cloth and drifting particles, spacious but focused frame.

[SUBJECT] in a three-quarter close-up, hair crossing the face, gaze partially hidden, the body turned through the wind, with cloth and fabric pushed diagonally across the frame, natural skin tones and calm expression.

[LAYOUT FAMILY] composition with white cloth, black hair, and one red ribbon or crimson streak moving through foreground and background, controlled blur, bokeh, and atmospheric fragments, not a fashion studio setup. If the theme is warrior-like, add armor plates, a weapon shaft, a mask, or ruined architecture as structural diagonals. If the theme is wuxia or Chinese ancient landscape, extend the frame with mountain gates, bamboo, pavilions, tiled eaves, river mist, or fortress walls. If the image leans like a battle key visual, let metallic reflections, sparks, and crowd haze create the background pressure.

Short title or micro caption in a quiet corner, minimal serif or typewriter feel, one strong red accent and otherwise ivory, black, warm skin, and pale sky, with film grain and soft highlight bloom.

Intimate cinematic mood, poetic and tense, editorial but not commercial; avoid static headshot, beauty-ad polish, neon, anime, glossy retouching, logo treatment, and heavy decorative clutter.
```
