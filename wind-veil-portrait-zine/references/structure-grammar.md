# Structure Grammar

Use this file when reverse-engineering a reference, diagnosing drift, or preserving the wind-swept portrait system.

## Visual DNA

- Vertical poster, usually 3:4 or 4:5.
- Horizontal 16:9 or 21:9 key visual posters are valid when the user wants a scene-rich wuxia main visual.
- One dominant human subject, usually close-cropped.
- Wind and cloth are the composition engine, not just decoration.
- The face is often partially veiled by hair or fabric.
- Foreground motion streaks and background bokeh create depth.
- A single accent color, often red or crimson, carries motion or emotional pressure.
- Matte photo texture, soft highlight bloom, and shallow depth of field are essential.
- Heroic combat variants may add armor mass, masks, talisman plates, weapon silhouettes, torn banners, sparks, or ruin architecture while keeping the portrait readable.
- Backgrounds can shift from open sky to battlefield haze, city ruins, temple walls, or broken scaffold forms, but they should stay soft enough to support the subject.
- Wuxia landscape variants may add tiled eaves, pavilions, mountain gates, bamboo groves, stone bridges, river mist, fortress walls, carved wood, lacquer, silk tassels, and ink-wash mountains as part of the same system.
- Reflective-battle variants may add wet lacquer armor, metallic shine, teal or cyan reflections, ember bokeh, orange-gold glow, and layered crowd silhouettes to build a more abstract battle atmosphere.

## Reference Extraction

Extract:

- subject scale and crop
- gaze direction and whether the face is frontal, three-quarter, or turned away
- wind direction and cloth path
- how much of the face is obscured by hair or fabric
- foreground blur, particle density, and background softness
- red ribbon or accent placement
- whether the image feels intimate, heroic, sorrowful, ritual, or free
- armor weight and whether it creates a diagonal or protective shell around the body
- mask, mouth guard, talisman strip, or face-scarring placement
- weapon angle, banner flow, or foreground obstruction path
- whether the environment feels like sky, siege, ruin, procession, or duel
- whether the environment feels like mountain gate, cliff path, bamboo courtyard, riverside pavilion, ancient city pass, or river crossing
- how the metallic surfaces catch light, and whether the image leans gold, teal, violet, ember-orange, or smoky black
- whether crowd figures are distinct or dissolved into haze and bokeh

## Composition Rules

- Keep one subject cluster only.
- Let the motion cross the face but not erase it.
- Use diagonal lines and layered streaks to make the frame feel in motion.
- Preserve enough empty or defocused area so the face remains the anchor.
- Use short text as a quiet physical element, not a poster headline.
- Let armor plates, scabbards, staff shafts, or banners inherit the same diagonal logic as cloth so the frame feels structurally organized.
- If the body is masked or armored, keep one eye or one facial plane clean enough to read emotion.
- When landscape elements are present, make roofs, pavilions, gates, mountains, and mist follow the same flow logic as cloth and weapon arcs.
- When reflective metal is present, let highlights break into sparks and ripples rather than flat chrome.
- When crowd silhouettes appear, reduce them to atmosphere so the hero remains the anchor.

## Style Drift Corrections

If it becomes a studio beauty shot:

- add stronger wind, cropped motion streaks, and more foreground obstruction
- break symmetry and remove clean background clarity

If it becomes too chaotic:

- reduce particle count and keep one main wind direction
- simplify to a single cloth path and one accent color

If it becomes too static:

- widen the flowing fabric or ribbon arc
- move the subject off-center and introduce stronger diagonal blur

If it becomes commercial:

- remove brand-like typography, glossy retouching, and product-light styling
- restore matte grain and editorial tension

If it becomes too generic fantasy:

- reduce toy-like costume detail and make the materials feel weathered, heavy, and photographed
- keep one real human facial anchor even when armor or masks dominate

If it becomes a tourist scene:

- remove postcard symmetry and flat scenic over-description
- fuse the scenery with wind, cloth, and weapon motion so it still reads as a poster

If it becomes too synthetic or game-like:

- soften the metal with grime, reflection breakup, and photographic bloom
- replace clean CGI surfaces with wet lacquer, embers, dust, and real shadow
