# Tableau Symbol System

Use symbolism as a visual grammar, not as a list of mystical props. Select one primary symbol, one material verb, and at most one secondary symbol.

## Concept Mapping

| Theme | Primary threshold | Material verb | Optional secondary symbol |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| memory | mirror, wet glass, old frame | duplicates, fogs, misaligns | bird or date mark |
| waiting | doorway, horizon, eclipse | suspends, slows, holds | bare branch |
| rebirth | arch, waterline, empty garment | emerges, sheds, ignites | pale crown |
| grief | black pool, closed frame | sinks, stains, evaporates | distant bird |
| identity | mirror, split pane | reflects, fractures, reverses | eye or veil |
| exile | narrow opening, severe horizon | recedes, erodes, drifts | suspended stone |
| devotion | altar-like frame, circle | kneels, gathers, illuminates | simple branch |
| silence | empty portal, fog field | erases, muffles, absorbs | tiny black disc |
| conflict | broken pane, divided waterline | collides, tears, burns | rust-red thread |
| return | door in landscape, reflective path | reconnects, clears, roots | bird flock |

## Primary Symbols

### Frame / Door / Arch

Meaning: boundary, decision, passage, missing architecture.

Physical rules:

- Show material thickness, weight, support, contact shadow, or buried base.
- The figure must stand in a meaningful relation to it.
- Keep the opening simple; ornate doors weaken the grammar.

### Mirror / Waterline / Glass

Meaning: double, memory, disagreement, temporal split.

Physical rules:

- Align perspective and anatomy before introducing distortion.
- Reflections inherit light direction.
- Rain, condensation, scratches, and ripples can obscure but not erase all structure.

### White Garment / Veil

Meaning: vulnerable body, absence, unfinished identity, ceremonial skin.

Physical rules:

- Show seams, frayed hems, wet weight, wrinkles, and translucency.
- Avoid royal gowns, polished couture, lace overload, and bridal coding unless requested.

### Smoke / Fire / Ash

Meaning: disappearance, signal, conversion, irreversible time.

Physical rules:

- Smoke follows airflow and light; fire requires a visible source or occupied object.
- Localize the bright chromatic event.
- No explosive spectacle.

### Eye / Eclipse / Halo

Meaning: witness, blocked knowledge, pause, distant force.

Physical rules:

- Use one disc or eye-like form, not repeated occult decoration.
- Keep scale deliberate: tiny witness, face-scale mask, or monumental sky event.
- Avoid copying specific religious emblems.

### Bird / Branch / Crown

Meaning: departure, witness, growth, temporary authority.

Physical rules:

- Use as a secondary gesture attached to body or frame.
- Prefer one branch or a sparse flock over ornamental abundance.
- Crowns should look handmade from branch, wire, ash, or paper.

## Material Verbs

- `crosses`: subject physically passes a threshold
- `becomes`: one material transitions continuously into another
- `reflects`: a second state appears in a coherent reflective plane
- `erases`: smoke, fog, rain, or exposure removes part of the subject
- `holds`: the figure stabilizes an impossible object or state
- `suspends`: gravity is visibly countered by wire, architecture, water, or composition
- `fractures`: a pane or reflection divides one subject into controlled segments
- `occupies`: an element fills a frame, garment, vessel, or absence

Avoid vague verbs such as `evokes`, `channels`, or `embodies` in the final image prompt unless followed by a visible action.

## Symbol Budget

A strong scene usually contains:

- one figure or one trace of a figure
- one threshold geometry
- one material transformation
- zero or one secondary organic sign
- zero or one chromatic event

When a scene exceeds this budget, remove symbols in reverse order: typography, secondary organic sign, atmospheric effect, then extra figure. Preserve the threshold and transformation.
