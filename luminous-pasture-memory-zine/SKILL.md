# Luminous Pasture Memory Zine

Turn the user's content into both:

1. a final image-generation prompt, and
2. a generated raster image made from that prompt.

## Source Grammar

Use this visual grammar as the style core:

- **Scene base:** tree roots, bark, shrubs, winter branches, grass, pasture edge, stream source, or mossy ground, often mixed between color photo and grayscale xerox.
- **Light behavior:** small warm white glowing specks scattered across branches, bark, leaves, paper edges, and image seams, like fireflies or tiny reflective glints.
- **Paper collage:** large white poster sheet, translucent vellum, perforated stamp-edge paper, torn green paper strip, pale beige ruled note, mint tape, and layered photo rectangles.
- **Photo system:** one main color nature photograph, one gray embossed/xerox nature panel, one tiny stamp-like green leaf or seedling photo.
- **Typography:** loose blue handwritten English poem lines, Chinese handwritten note on lined paper, signature such as `RECORDER`, and sparse pencil-like captions.
- **Composition:** layered scrapbook-poster with large white or pale sky-paper areas, overlapping photo fragments, visible tape, torn edges, and gentle asymmetry.
- **Mood:** pastoral memory, field recorder, quiet invitation, "you come too", spring water, waiting, return, soft outdoor archive.

## Mode Policy

Use **Standard Mode** for all generation. Compile only the visual details that should become pixels. Do not include analysis prose, source filenames, or process notes inside the final image prompt.

## Standard Mode Prompt Compiler

Every prompt must describe a single finished raster poster image, not a UI, moodboard, or clean layout template.

### First-Principles Fields

1. **Canvas and Surface**
   - Vertical 3:5 poster by default.
   - White or pale cream paper field, pale blue sky-paper field, or a full-frame nature photo partly covered by white paper.
   - Keep 35%-65% readable paper or pale field space even when using full nature-photo background.
   - Use scanned-paper texture, soft grain, translucent overlays, and handmade collage depth.

2. **Nature Memory Anchor**
   - Convert the user's theme into one natural field-memory subject: tree root, bark, branch silhouette, green leaf, moss, grass, stream source, pasture path, seedling, stone, or water mark.
   - Use one main color photo rectangle or large background crop as the emotional anchor.
   - The subject should feel observed and remembered, not staged.

3. **Layered Photo System**
   - Add a grayscale xerox or embossed-looking nature panel with stamp-like perforated edges.
   - Add one small postage-stamp mini photo, often a green leaf or tiny plant on dark ground.
   - Optionally add a second translucent branch layer, faded gray background branches, or a cropped nature texture strip.
   - Let photos overlap imperfectly; keep visible white gaps and paper edges.

4. **Handwriting and Notes**
   - Add blue handwritten English poem lines across the lower or left paper area.
   - Add a beige ruled notebook scrap with Chinese handwriting, usually in the upper-right or upper-left.
   - Include a short handwritten Chinese sentence on green torn paper or directly on white paper.
   - Add a small `RECORDER` signature or field-note caption near the top or lower right.
   - Use short exact phrases only; dense handwriting can be semi-legible.

5. **Tape, Tear, and Stamp Details**
   - Add mint-green tape diagonally on a corner or over a photo edge.
   - Add torn sage-green paper strip under notes or behind a mini photo.
   - Use perforated stamp edges, notebook hole punches, torn paper fibers, translucent vellum, and misaligned scraps.
   - These materials should look tactile, not flat vector shapes.

6. **Luminous Accent Logic**
   - Scatter many tiny warm white-yellow glints across nature and paper.
   - Glints should be pin-sized and irregular, not large starbursts.
   - A few can sit on bark, leaves, photo panels, tape, and page seams.
   - Preserve the quiet green/gray/white palette; light is the main accent.

7. **Reproduction Texture**
   - Use xerox softness, risograph grain, film dust, scan noise, paper fibers, pale wash, and low contrast.
   - Allow a soft dreamy overprint, but maintain paper collage clarity.
   - Avoid glossy photographic realism as the only texture.

8. **Emotional Temperature**
   - Quiet, inviting, nostalgic, pastoral, field-recorded, springlike, intimate, handmade.
   - The viewer should feel a remembered walk near tree roots or water before reading the text.

9. **Hard Avoids**
   - Avoid commercial travel poster, UI, digital moodboard, glossy stock-photo collage, neon fantasy forest, fairy illustration, cute scrapbook overload, clean vector stationery, sharp corporate layout, 3D render, cinematic depth-of-field drama, saturated rainbow colors, or long perfectly readable text blocks.

### Standard Prompt Shape

Write the final Standard Mode prompt as four compact paragraphs:

1. canvas, paper/nature base, negative space, overall collage arrangement
2. main nature photo anchor, grayscale xerox panel, mini leaf/stamp photo, layering
3. handwriting system, Chinese note, blue poem text, tape, torn green paper, `RECORDER` caption
4. luminous specks, reproduction texture, mood, lighting, and avoid-list

Prefer concrete visible instructions over abstract style words. If the user supplies no text, invent one short Chinese note and one short English poem fragment.

## Variation Engine

Before writing the prompt, choose one option from each axis. For multiple images, vary at least three axes per image.

## Workflow

1. Determine mode.
   - Use Standard Mode.

2. Parse the user's content.
   - Identify the core nature subject, memory, mood, exact text if supplied, and any reference image role.
   - Convert abstract ideas into one field-memory anchor: tree root, branch, leaf, water, pasture, bark, or seedling.
   - If no text is supplied, invent a short Chinese line such as `我不会去太久——你也来吧。` and a short English fragment such as `YOU COME TOO`.

3. Select a variation recipe.
   - Pick layout, nature anchor, photo treatment, handwriting mode, paper prop, light mode, and mood.
   - Keep the poster layered but not chaotic. Preserve clear paper regions for handwritten text.
   - If recent outputs used the same central tree-root photo, switch to stamp-led, full-bark, branch-border, or blue-sky-paper grammar.

4. Write the final image prompt.
   - Use the four-paragraph Standard Prompt Shape.
   - Specify where the main photo, xerox panel, ruled note, tape, torn strip, mini photo, and handwriting sit.
   - Keep exact in-image text short; use dense micro-handwriting only as visual texture.

5. Generate the image.
   - Use the built-in image generation capability by default.
   - Do not stop after prompt-only unless the user explicitly asks for prompt-only.
   - If the result becomes a fantasy forest, generic scrapbook, or glossy photo collage, tighten the paper, xerox, stamp-edge, handwritten, and restrained glint constraints and regenerate once.

6. Return the image and prompt.

## Negative Constraints

Always avoid:

- commercial nature poster, travel ad, wellness brand ad, logo/CTA layout
- clean UI, digital moodboard, website mockup, vector stationery layout
- glossy stock-photo collage with no paper texture
- fairy fantasy forest, magical creature scene, neon glow, cyberpunk colors
- cute sticker overload, kawaii scrapbook, anime illustration
- 3D rendering, cinematic spotlight, dramatic shallow depth of field
- saturated rainbow palette, heavy purple/blue gradients, high-contrast poster design
- perfectly typeset long paragraphs, clean corporate typography, generic lorem ipsum blocks
- too many unrelated photos or decorative scraps

## Quality Gate

Before finalizing, check:

- Did the prompt create a vertical layered paper collage rather than a clean digital layout?
- Is there one clear nature-memory anchor such as tree root, bark, branch, leaf, pasture, water, moss, or seedling?
- Are color photo and grayscale xerox/embossed nature layers both represented when appropriate?
- Are perforated stamp paper, lined notebook scrap, torn green paper, tape, or white poster sheet visible?
- Are blue handwritten English poem lines or a short English fragment included?
- Is there Chinese handwriting on a ruled note or torn paper?
- Are tiny warm glowing specks scattered irregularly across nature and paper?
- Does the palette stay restrained: green, gray, white, beige, pale blue, mint tape, warm light?
- Does the poster feel like a field recorder memory page, not a fantasy forest or commercial ad?
- Did the prompt avoid UI, stock-photo collage, neon, 3D, cute scrapbook, and long clean text?
- Did you actually generate the image?
