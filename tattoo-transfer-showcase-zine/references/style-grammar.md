# Style Grammar

Use this reference when matching the tattoo showcase structure or correcting drift.

## Source Fusion

From Minimal Zine Poster v0.1:

- large quiet paper field
- one clear visual anchor
- sparse typography
- visible but restrained chromatic accent
- matte scanned-paper finish
- poetic diary/editorial mood

From the tattoo reference set:

- horizontal presentation sheet
- applied tattoo photo on skin at left
- clean isolated tattoo artwork on paper at right
- short poetic line below the art
- optional small transfer size and invented studio mark
- fine line, watercolor bloom, ink dots, tiny drips, and airy handmade marks

The reference set also establishes a four-part mark grammar: a readable outer
silhouette, a sparse structural skeleton, visible dry-brush or colored-pencil
material, and a few detached pigment accents. Keep these layers distinct so a
model can add texture without losing the tattoo's shape.

## Spatial Rules

- Use a wide canvas: 16:9 for cinematic product sheets, 4:3 for intimate catalog pages.
- Keep the right artwork panel calm: 55%-80% of that panel should remain blank paper.
- Let the left photo be soft and low contrast, but do not make the tattoo invisible.
- Avoid hard black panel borders. Use gentle separation through whitespace, paper tone, or a faint fold line.
- Keep body crops partial and tasteful: wrist, forearm, hand, ankle, waist, shoulder, collarbone, back, or neck detail.
- For a closer match, keep one shared vertical or diagonal axis across both panels; do not mirror, rotate, or redesign the specimen between the photo and paper sides.

## Material Rules

- Background: ivory paper, cream paper, off-white transfer backing, warm gray vellum, faint paper fibers.
- Photo: natural skin tone, soft daylight, linen/cotton clothing, shallow softness, no fashion-ad drama.
- Tattoo art: printable transfer ink, thin lines, watercolor wash, pencil sketch marks, tiny ink freckles, slight bleed.
- Use controlled imperfection: broken contours, 1-3 transparent washes, 2-5 detached pigment flecks, and occasional tapered drips. Avoid random texture everywhere.
- Separate color from structure: the darkest anchor line must remain readable, while echo colors and transparent washes touch only selected segments. See [color-line-system.md](color-line-system.md) for palette roles and line hierarchy.
- On skin, let pores remain visible through the pigment and remove every sticker edge, white halo, gloss, or cast shadow.
- When the crop is a hand, preserve the distinct finger count, knuckles, tendons, nails, and natural joint direction; align the tattoo to that anatomy instead of floating it on top.
- For multicolor work, use an anchor, bridge, counterpoint, and no more than two tiny sparks. See [hand-type-multicolor.md](hand-type-multicolor.md) for the full system.
- Typography: small serif, typewriter, thin sans, pencil handwriting, faint stamp-like size note.

## Motif Translation

Turn themes into tattooable marks, not illustrated scenes:

- "rain" -> falling line threads, tiny splash dots, translucent blue-gray wash
- "spring" -> green sprout vine, pink bud, yellow spark
- "cloud mountain" -> one mountain contour held by slow cloud lines
- "tropical plant" -> monstera, fern, palm rib, or staghorn fern silhouette trace
- "fish water cloud sea" -> one fish-line figure crossing a wave/cloud ribbon
- "firework" -> jellyfish-like colored filaments and small star sparks

## Text Boundaries

- Preserve exact user text if supplied, but keep it short and small.
- If inventing text, use one line only.
- Do not imitate or reproduce reference captions, brand names, signatures, or watermarks.
- Use fictional marks such as `field note 03`, `temporary transfer`, `8 x 3 cm`, or a made-up studio stamp only when it improves the sheet.

## Drift Corrections

- If the output becomes an ad: remove headline, CTA, price, and logo wording; describe it as a quiet archival transfer sheet.
- If the output becomes only tattoo art: require a left-side applied skin placement photo and right-side isolated transfer drawing.
- If the output is too dark: specify bright ivory paper, soft daylight, airy photo exposure, and clean printable ink.
- If the output is too cluttered: remove alternate stickers and keep one motif plus one caption.
- If the output copies reference text: replace all non-user text with invented small metadata.
- If the output is too generic: specify the silhouette, axis, branch count, focal node, and exact accent colors; adding more adjectives or realism will not improve structural fidelity.
- If the linework looks digital: restore pressure variation, dry pencil grain, broken joins, tapered endings, and one partial offset echo trace.
- If the text feels pasted on: reduce it to a short caption, add an optional smaller translation, and separate the size or edition metadata from the caption line.
