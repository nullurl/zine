# Cobalt Breath Poetry Recipe

Use this optional named recipe only when water, breath, or depth is the top-ranked relation in the poem's image map, when a supplied visual reference clearly uses this identity, or when the user explicitly requests it. A matching keyword alone is insufficient.

## Canonical Layout IDs

Choose exactly one ID and reproduce its geometry. Do not invent or rename a layout.

- `upper-text-lower-sea-strip`: poem in the upper third; one low ragged cobalt sea strip in the middle or lower-middle.
- `upper-text-lower-organic-pool`: poem upper-left or upper-center; one oval, leaf-like, or brush-masked pool below.
- `left-specimen-right-passage`: one small cobalt person/object specimen at left-middle; poem in a calm right column.
- `left-passage-tall-water-window`: short poem on the left; one tall torn cobalt bubble/depth window on the right.
- `upper-passage-lower-letter-sea`: poem upper-left; one wide lower cobalt field with a letter, page, rope, or hand partly submerged.
- `source-fragment-depth-axis`: one tiny source fragment near the top; one narrow diver/body vignette on a long depth axis below.
- `split-metaphor-relation`: poem on the left; one person-object relation inside a shared organic cobalt field on the right.

Forbidden substitutes: `ripple-title`, `centered-strip`, hanging rice-paper strip, scroll, wall gallery, framed rectangular illustration, ornate English title, calligraphy poster, or red seal.

## Structure

- Canvas: vertical `3:4` preferred, `4:5` allowed for feed delivery.
- Paper: warm ivory/oatmeal with visible fibers, specks, scratches, and matte scan softness.
- Quiet field: 58%-75%.
- Poem block: 4-8 short lines, about 10%-22%, usually upper-left, upper-center, or left-middle.
- Vignette: one 18%-32% cobalt duotone photograph/illustration with organic brush, torn, oval, strip, or tall-window boundary.
- Micro marks: one source line plus optional breath/depth/time notation, crosshair, or two tiny plus signs.

## Metaphor Choices

- breath and bubbles
- body and sea
- fear and depth
- courage and first movement
- writing and an endless paper trail
- memory and a submerged object
- growth and an unstable structure
- rescue and a small life changing one world

Choose one relation and remove every decorative object that does not support it.

Before choosing a person or diver, consider poem-specific nonhuman anchors such as a tide chart, shell, wet page, pier light, air pocket, fishing line, reflection, salt trace, or horizon notch. Use a body only when the poem makes it primary.

## Background Prompt

Use the normal four-paragraph compiler, but name and implement the selected canonical layout geometry, warm fibrous paper, one metaphor relation, and cobalt/gray-blue halftone reproduction. Require no text, no second hue, no clean rectangle, no hanging strip or scroll, no red seal, no ornate title, no full-bleed scene, no glossy depth, and no commercial hierarchy.

Typeset the exact poem afterward. Keep source/author smaller and lighter than the poem.
