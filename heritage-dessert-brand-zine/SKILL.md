# Heritage Dessert Brand Zine

Turn the user's content into both:

1. a final image-generation prompt, and
2. a generated raster image made from that prompt.

## Source Grammar

Use this visual identity:

- vertical 3:5 or 4:5 brand poster; occasional square/stacked layout only for brand-system boards
- warm family-restaurant atmosphere: terracotta tile grid, honey wood, rattan cane panels, bamboo sign frames, lantern glow, muted olive seating, dark wood tabletop
- Chinese dessert/tea-drink product focus: shaved ice, mango pomelo sago, taro balls, grass jelly, mung bean, pistachio, lotus seed, red bean, taro, seasonal fruit bowls
- brand-system overlays: corner English labels `FAMILY RESTAURANT` and romanized brand name, Chinese calligraphic logo, small seals, ingredient icons, packaging cards, fabric labels, receipt-like cards
- typography: large brush-style Chinese brand name, small serif/typewriter English, vertical Chinese product names, stamp labels, restrained bilingual captions
- materials: kraft paper, recycled cream paper, green product cards, linen cloth, rattan weaving, woodgrain pattern, ceramic bowls, metal spoon, warm lamp glow
- composition feels like restaurant brand-design documentation, not generic food photography
- optional social-media overlay: subtle white crop brackets and creator watermark, only when asked for Xiaohongshu/process-board feeling

The image should feel like a polished but tactile Chinese dessert brand identity zine: warm, handmade, editorial, material-rich, and restaurant-specific.

## Mode Policy

Use **Standard Mode** for all generation. Use the Standard Mode Prompt Compiler below to convert the user's content into compact, imageable prompts.

Use prompt-only output only when the user explicitly asks for prompt-only.

## Standard Mode Prompt Compiler

Default generation should compile only visible image instructions.

### First-Principles Fields

Every prompt must answer these rendering questions in this order:

1. **Canvas:** What is the frame?
   - vertical 3:5 or 4:5 brand poster by default; square only for identity-arrangement boards; no app UI unless requested.

2. **Brand Context:** What restaurant or dessert concept is being branded?
   - convert the user's idea into one Chinese dessert restaurant, family tea-dessert shop, seasonal shaved-ice menu item, ingredient set, packaging system, or interior signage moment.

3. **Scene/Surface:** What material world supports the brand?
   - choose one: terracotta tile wall, rattan screen and wood frame, warm booth interior, dark wood tabletop, cream paper board, kraft paper texture, wrinkled olive cloth, or bamboo sign frame.

4. **Product Anchor:** What food/product is visible?
   - one or several ceramic bowls, shaved ice, fruit dessert, tea drink, ingredient close-up, menu-card product illustration, or packaged dessert card. The product must support the brand identity, not dominate as generic food advertising.

5. **Brand Mark System:** What identity elements appear?
   - calligraphic Chinese logo; romanized English name; corner label such as `FAMILY RESTAURANT`; small seal stamps; ingredient icons; vertical Chinese product labels; optional brand-system caption.

6. **Layout Structure:** How are the elements arranged?
   - choose interior signage photo, product hero poster, material identity board, ingredient illustration board, packaging-card fan, textile label board, menu system, or product lineup.

7. **Typography Logic:** How should text behave?
   - use short readable text blocks only. Prefer: large Chinese calligraphy logo; top-left serif `FAMILY RESTAURANT`; top-right romanized name; vertical Chinese product labels; tiny English subtitles. Do not use long paragraphs.

8. **Color Logic:** What palette controls the mood?
   - warm terracotta, honey brown, cream paper, muted olive green, tea beige, deep wood black-brown, small fruit colors. Keep saturation earthy except the dessert itself.

9. **Texture/Reproduction:** What makes it tactile?
   - paper grain, tile grout, rattan weave, wood scratches, fabric wrinkles, lamp bloom, aged print noise, ink bleed, shallow phone-photo softness.

10. **Emotional Temperature:** What should the viewer feel first?
   - family restaurant warmth, handmade seasonal taste, nostalgic Chinese dessert shop, restrained brand craft, slow-food care, everyday hospitality.

11. **Hard Avoids:** What must not appear?
   - generic fast-food ad, luxury western fine-dining campaign, plastic neon milk tea poster, sterile product mockup, clean UI, cute cartoon mascot overload, overfilled menu design, irrelevant brand names.

### Standard Prompt Shape

Write the final Standard Mode prompt as four compact paragraphs:

1. canvas + brand context + material scene/surface
2. product anchor + layout structure + brand marks
3. typography + palette + tactile texture
4. mood + social-overlay policy + avoid-list

Do not describe the reference images or the skill. Describe the final image to generate.

## Workflow

1. Parse the user's content.
   - Identify the restaurant/dessert concept, product, flavor, season, target mood, and any exact brand name.
   - If no brand name is supplied, invent a short Chinese dessert-shop name plus romanized subtitle.
   - If the user supplies exact text, preserve it where possible but keep text short.

2. Select a variation recipe.
   - Choose layout, scene/surface, product anchor, brand mark mode, texture mode, and palette mode from the Variation Engine.
   - For batches, make each image use a materially different layout family.

3. Write the final image prompt.
   - Use the Standard Prompt Shape.
   - Specify visible typography: Chinese logo, top-left/top-right English labels, product name, seal stamps, and optional watermark/crop brackets.
   - Keep product, materials, and brand marks balanced. Do not let the image become a generic dessert ad.

4. Generate the image.
   - Use the available image generation capability by default.
   - Do not stop after prompt-only unless the user asks for prompt-only.
   - If the result becomes generic food photography, a clean menu, or a neon milk-tea ad, tighten the prompt and regenerate once with stronger `brand identity board`, `rattan/tile/wood material`, `Chinese calligraphy logo`, and `tactile editorial restaurant zine` wording.

5. Return the image and prompt.

## Negative Constraints

Always avoid:

- generic fast-food ad or franchise poster
- neon milk-tea visual, plastic cup campaign, cute mascot overload
- sterile product mockup, clean UI, app screenshot, website layout
- western luxury fine-dining ad, black-gold premium campaign
- busy menu board with many prices and items
- unrelated logo marks or famous existing brands
- glossy 3D render, vector-only flat design
- anime/cartoon food illustration unless explicitly requested
- long unreadable paragraphs or too many text blocks
- overexposed stock food photography with no brand-system elements
