# Placement and Lighting

Use this reference for body portraits and paired proofs.

## Placement Map

### Upper Arm and Deltoid

- Crop from shoulder to mid-forearm or shoulder to elbow.
- Align vertical to the humerus; let a burst cross the deltoid curve.
- Preserve shoulder roundness, armpit transition, elbow direction, and pores.
- Frame with black sleeveless fabric or hair, without hiding the tattoo.

### Forearm

- Align stems, snakes, birds, or long tails with the forearm axis.
- Show wrist or elbow context so orientation remains believable.
- Reduce foreshortening; avoid floating tattoo art on a generic skin rectangle.

### Shoulder Blade and Upper Back

- Preserve neck, trapezius, scapula ridge, spine, and shoulder width.
- A centered motif may cross the spine lightly; a shoulder burst should bend
  over one scapula.
- Use thin straps or draped fabric as a frame; no impossible shoulder anatomy.

### Waist and Side Torso

- Follow rib and waist curvature with a diagonal flight or fine trailing line.
- Keep the navel, rib edge, or garment boundary as orientation context.
- Avoid distorted torso proportions or overly revealing framing.

### Collarbone

- Use a short diagonal stem, wing, or orbit loop below the clavicle.
- Keep scale small and avoid covering the neck or face.

### Thigh

- Use vertical totem or wrapped coil aligned with muscle direction.
- Include knee or garment edge for scale; avoid plastic, featureless skin.

## Portrait Families

### Black-Void Portrait

Large black hair and clothing masses surround one lit skin plane. Light is soft,
directional, and low key. The face is cropped, turned away, or softly obscured.
Use for snake, bird, octopus, and dense black motifs.

### Gray-Daylight Portrait

Soft window light, warm gray room or distant waterfront, shallow background,
natural skin, and quiet cotton fabric. Use for floral and lighter butterfly work.

### Warm Rose Low Light

Subtle rose-beige skin light against a charcoal-black frame. Keep saturation
low and shadows lifted. Use for intimate shoulder or arm placement without
fashion-ad drama.

## Skin Integration Block

Use this logic directly in prompts:

```text
The tattoo follows the body's curvature and anatomical axis. Ink is matte and
embedded in the skin: pores, folds, and subtle tonal variation remain visible
through gray wash; black pigment softens slightly at the edges while the gesture
skeleton stays sharp. No decal border, white halo, gloss, raised surface, cast
shadow, duplicated mark, or rectangular transfer film.
```

## Anatomical Gate

Before accepting a body image, check:

- limb count and joint direction;
- shoulder, scapula, spine, rib, wrist, and elbow continuity;
- natural skin texture without waxy retouching;
- tattoo bends with, rather than ignores, the surface;
- clothing and hair do not merge into the anatomy;
- crop does not depend on a recognizable face or identity.
