# Prompt Recipes

Use these as structural recipes, not wording to copy verbatim.

## Dark Snake Arm

- Mode: body-portrait, 4:5 vertical.
- Placement: upper arm to forearm, vertical S-axis.
- Motif: one snake, two heavy bends, five broken leaves.
- Portrait: black void, dark hair/clothing, softly lit skin.
- Accent: none.

## Bird Sequence Back

- Mode: body-portrait or paper-specimen, square.
- Placement: centered upper back or vertical paper center.
- Motif: one anchor bird, two lighter echoes, moon wash, three orbit lines.
- Portrait: gray daylight, thin black straps, identity cropped.
- Accent: optional tiny cobalt node.

## Butterfly Shoulder Fracture

- Mode: body-portrait, square.
- Placement: scapula or deltoid shoulder burst.
- Motif: one asymmetric butterfly, one ghost wing, thin orbit loop.
- Portrait: soft gray daylight or warm rose low light.
- Accent: none or a small vermilion circle on the paper version only.

## Octopus Arm Orbit

- Mode: body-portrait, 4:5 vertical.
- Placement: upper arm, tentacles follow arm curvature.
- Motif: one head, three dominant loops, two ghost lines, sparse suckers.
- Portrait: black void or warm rose low light.
- Accent: none.

## Minimal Lily Specimen

- Mode: paper-specimen, 3:4 vertical.
- Placement: center or slightly lower-middle, 55%-70% quiet paper.
- Motif: one lily, one bud, one stem, four bristle leaves.
- Accent: optional cobalt wash occupying 8%-12% of the cluster.

## Abstract Accent Totem

- Mode: paper-specimen, 3:5 vertical.
- Placement: narrow central gesture occupying 18%-30% of canvas width.
- Motif: smoke, jellyfish, mountain-current, or abstract breath.
- Accent: one vermilion circle, cobalt cloud, or muted gold dust.
- Minimal Zine: no text by default; paper grain and one anchor do the work.

## Correction Order

1. Fix anatomy or body-surface mapping.
2. Restore one dominant axis and remove extra subjects.
3. Rebuild black/wash/open-substrate separation.
4. Reduce trajectory lines and splatter.
5. Remove extra colors, text, or commercial styling.
