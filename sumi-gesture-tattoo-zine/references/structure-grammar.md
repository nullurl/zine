# Structure Grammar

Use this reference when analyzing supplied images or building a new composition.

## Repeated Reference Rules

The source family alternates between two presentations of the same visual
language:

| Variable | Paper specimen | Body portrait |
|---|---|---|
| Canvas | square or vertical | square or 4:5 vertical |
| Ground | bright fibrous white | black, charcoal, or soft gray |
| Anchor | centered vertical/diagonal mark | mark aligned to exposed skin plane |
| Empty area | 45%-75% paper | 35%-65% dark frame or bare skin |
| Ink | carbon black + gray wash | same geometry, slightly softened |
| Motion | loops, whips, drips, long tails | follows arm, shoulder, back, or waist |
| Text | none | none |

Repeated subjects are snakes with foliage, birds in flight, butterflies,
octopus tentacles, lilies, and abstract smoke or jellyfish gestures. Repetition
establishes a system, not a requirement to copy those exact drawings.

## Reference Analysis Card

Record one short value for each field before compiling:

- output mode and canvas ratio;
- exposed-skin or paper shape;
- dominant axis and direction;
- primary subject and echo count;
- number and location of black masses;
- wash zones and open channels;
- trajectory lines and terminal direction;
- substrate/background value;
- light direction and contrast;
- accent color, if any.

## Composition Families

### Vertical Totem

One S-curve or stem carries a snake, bird sequence, butterfly pair, or flower.
The top begins lighter and the bottom resolves into a long taper or drip. Best
for upper arm, forearm, spine, thigh, and vertical paper.

### Diagonal Flight

Two or three birds or butterflies follow one rising diagonal. One large subject
anchors the sequence; orbit curves link movement without enclosing every form.
Best for shoulder blade, waist, side torso, and square paper.

### Wrapped Coil

One snake, octopus, or abstract ribbon creates two or three overlapping loops.
Keep one open center channel and one clearly readable head or focal node. Best
for upper arm, shoulder, thigh, and centered paper specimens.

### Shoulder Burst

One butterfly, flower, or winged abstraction spreads from the scapula or deltoid
with long asymmetric strokes. Keep the neck and shoulder contour visible. Best
for square body portraits and square paper specimens.

### Back Constellation

A centered flower/bird/moon node anchors a thin horizontal or diagonal line
system across the shoulder blades. Keep the spine readable and the lower tail
sparse. Best for upper back and wide square compositions.

## Scale Rules

- Paper motif occupies 25%-55% of the canvas, not the entire page.
- Body tattoo occupies 35%-75% of the exposed skin shape but does not become a
  full sleeve unless explicitly requested.
- One focal black mass is roughly twice the visual weight of any support mass.
- Long filaments may travel beyond the motif, but should not touch every edge.
- Keep one continuous quiet region large enough to read at thumbnail scale.

## Precision Controls

Use these directly in prompts when matching a reference:

- same primary axis and focal-node count;
- one readable subject family, no additional creature;
- dense black limited to named nodes;
- preserve white channels through the body of the motif;
- no mirrored duplicate, alternate design, or decorative frame;
- paper and body variants share geometry and orientation;
- no copied signature, text, watermark, or identity.
