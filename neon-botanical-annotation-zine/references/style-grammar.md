# Neon Botanical Annotation Zine Style Grammar

Use this file when a request asks to reverse-engineer references, merge visual grammars, or correct style drift.

## Reverse-Engineered Reference Grammar

The reference set shares this visual language:

- real low-light plant photography as the base, often dusk, night, shade, or deep green garden
- one flower or plant dominates: pink hollyhock, red lily, daisy cluster, yellow-red lily, pale lily, or glass bottle in grass
- overlays sit on top like early-digital personal zine graphics
- mark vocabulary: cyan squares, magenta numbered circles, white dotted grids, pixel line drawings, thin rectangular frames, arrows, translucent circles, color chips, stars, music notes, script labels
- type is short and decorative: plant name, season word, tiny parenthetical notes, one sentence at most
- color logic: dark green/black photo field plus high-chroma cyan, magenta, white, red, or pale blue annotation
- mood: melancholy garden diary, amateur field note, night-blooming flower index, early web scrapbook

Do not copy exact reference text. Extract mark behavior, color relationships, and composition.

## Minimal Zine Fusion

Bring from Minimal Zine:

- one clear anchor, not a general scene
- restrained mark density
- short text
- a visible high-chroma accent
- negative space, interpreted as dark foliage, black background, sky, or quiet photo area
- print/scan softness, film grain, misregistration, and diary mood

Change from Minimal Zine:

- use a photographic canvas instead of aged paper by default
- allow wider landscape-style references, but output can remain portrait unless the user requests wide
- let annotations act as typography and color anchor
- keep one real plant photo anchor larger than Minimal Zine's tiny specimen

## Overlay Vocabulary

Use specific overlay details:

- numbered circles: magenta or red, 1-3 markers only
- dots: white, pale blue, cyan, or magenta, arranged as a grid, orbit, trail, or scattered field
- thin frames: white rectangle, inset crop box, callout square, or double frame
- arrows and leaders: white or cyan line, one or two only
- pixel art: simple white line flower icon or blocky text
- stars: tiny four-point or five-point marks, sparse
- labels: script plant name, tiny monospaced note, parenthetical season word
- color chips: translucent rounded rectangle panel with 3-5 circles or squares

Avoid generic "graphic elements" without naming the marks.

## Composition Ratios

Default portrait output:

- plant anchor: 20%-45% of image
- quiet photo field: 40%-70%
- overlays: 2%-12%
- text: under 6%

Reference-like wide output:

- plant anchor: 25%-55%
- quiet photo field: 30%-60%
- overlays: 3%-15%

Keep the main flower readable at thumbnail size. Do not cover the flower center with too many marks.

## Photo Treatment

Use:

- real flower photography
- low-light exposure or backlit sky
- soft film grain
- slight motion blur or handheld softness when appropriate
- dark green, black, or blue shadow fields
- shallow-to-medium depth of field

Avoid:

- polished stock-photo brightness
- studio product macro
- fully illustrated flowers
- clean mobile-app interface
- heavy cyberpunk glow

## Style Drift Corrections

If the output becomes clean scrapbook:

- remove paper scraps, washi, Polaroids, lace, and stickers
- add "digital overlay marks printed over a dark photograph"

If the output becomes UI:

- remove app panels, buttons, navigation, and interface hierarchy
- add "loose zine annotations, imperfect registration, not a functional interface"

If the output is too dark:

- brighten the flower anchor while keeping the background shadowy
- use cyan or white annotations to guide the eye

If overlays disappear:

- specify exact count and placement of dots, frames, numbered circles, or labels
- increase overlay opacity and accent share to 5%-10%

If text becomes too much:

- keep only one plant name and one tiny season note
