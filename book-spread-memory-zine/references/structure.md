# Book Spread Memory Structure

## Reverse-engineered source DNA

- A real open book, notebook, or sketchbook seen from above.
- Two-page spread with visible spine, page creases, and page edges.
- Warm paper, cream paper, gray paper, or lightly aged handmade paper.
- One side often carries photos or collage scraps; the other side often carries handwriting or a larger text block.
- Some spreads place a single image block on each page and let the writing occupy the remaining space.
- The book often sits on a rough natural surface: stone, asphalt, leather, concrete, or dark fabric.
- Shadows are soft and physical, not studio-clean.
- The overall mood is intimate, archival, literary, and personal.

## Layout families

- **image-left/text-right:** left page holds one or two photo blocks, right page holds handwriting or a long quote.
- **text-left/image-right:** reverse of the above, often stronger for portrait or cinematic material.
- **paired-pages:** both pages hold related images, with handwritten notes used sparingly.
- **central-essay:** one strong image or torn-paper field sits on one page, while the facing page carries a large handwritten passage.
- **three-frame spread:** one page holds two smaller photos, the other page holds one larger photo or text block.
- **catalog spread:** both pages use small framed photos with labels, dates, and tiny archival notes.

## Surface recipes

- Stone or terrazzo background for a grounded documentary feel.
- Dark asphalt or gravel for a rough, cinematic diary feel.
- Beige leather or fabric for a warm domestic feel.
- Pale tabletop or linen for a softer, quieter spread.

## Page recipes

- Left page as image anchor, right page as handwritten essay.
- Left page as faded text field, right page as photo collage.
- One large image block on one page, one smaller supporting block on the other.
- One torn paper patch crossing the gutter when the composition needs more tension.
- Small tape strips, corner tabs, or translucent overlays used sparingly.

## Handwriting rules

- Use Chinese handwriting or mixed handwritten/typed captions when the source suggests it.
- Keep the writing intimate, uneven, and slightly compressed.
- Prefer short paragraphs, poetic lines, archive notes, or quote fragments.
- Avoid long clean body text blocks unless the source clearly needs them.

## Prompt recipe

1. Canvas and book state: ratio, camera angle, paper tone, surface.
2. Page structure: which page gets photos, which page gets handwriting, how the gutter works.
3. Handwriting and marks: script style, caption length, small labels, tape, faint lines.
4. Mood and avoids: diary tone plus strict negative constraints.

## Good hard avoids

- Poster hierarchy
- Digital UI layout
- Clean mockup shadows
- Neon or glitch color
- Full-bleed single image without a spread
- Dense scrapbook clutter
- Overly polished typography

## Example prompt skeleton

```text
Top-down open sketchbook spread on rough stone, warm cream paper, visible spine, soft natural shadow.

Left page holds one or two framed photo blocks; right page holds handwritten Chinese notes and a small caption. Keep the page balance quiet and intentional.

Add a few faint pencil lines, small tape strips, and one tiny label near the margin. Let the handwriting feel personal and slightly uneven.

Keep it intimate, archival, and flat; avoid poster layout, glossy mockup, neon, dense collage clutter, and full-bleed digital polish.
```
