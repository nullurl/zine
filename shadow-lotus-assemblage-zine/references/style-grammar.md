# Style Grammar

Use this file when reverse-engineering a reference, diagnosing style drift, or preserving the dark devotional assemblage system.

## Visual DNA

- Vertical 3:4 or 4:5 poster, flat scanned or printed rather than 3D.
- Dark base field: black ink, smoky olive, deep green, charcoal, worn bronze-black, or low-lit paper.
- Clear layer stack:
  - background: smoky texture, paper fibers, grain, dim stains
  - material: vellum, torn paper, lotus leaves, translucent panels, smoke, gold thread
  - glyph: vertical calligraphy, seals, micro labels, scattered ideographic fragments
  - subject: cropped Buddha face, statue head, sculpture hand, lotus bloom, shrine fragment
- One dominant subject controls the image. It may be oversized and partially cut by the frame.
- Secondary botanicals and material scraps support the subject; they do not create a cheerful scrapbook.
- Typography feels printed, brushed, stamped, or worn into the surface.
- Accent palette is narrow: cinnabar, oxidized gold, lotus pink, aged cream, or deep red panel.

## Reference Extraction

Extract:

- canvas ratio and whether the composition is edge-cropped, centered, or panel-led
- background tone, texture density, and empty-space ratio
- main subject identity, scale, crop, and material quality
- lotus, leaf, vellum, smoke, ribbon, and paper-layer placement
- calligraphy direction, density, contrast, and relationship to the subject
- seal color, seal count, and whether seals act as anchors or small marks
- geometric panels, circles, arrows, blocks, and whether they should remain abstract or become material overlays
- warm accent hue and where it appears

## Composition Rules

- Let the subject carry scale, not headline text.
- Use vertical flow: calligraphy, smoke, lotus stems, or ribbons can guide the eye up and down.
- Crop sculpture boldly when using edge-statue layouts; keep the face calm and grayscale.
- Use translucent overlays as physical material, not digital UI panels.
- When adapting diagram references, remove diagram labels and translate blocks/circles/arrows into vellum, seals, red panels, moon disks, or shadow windows.
- Keep the layout ceremonial and editorial, not temple-tourism photography.

## Style Drift Corrections

If it becomes a clean infographic:

- remove labels, axis lines, and tutorial wording
- convert geometry into vellum, seals, or ink blocks
- add print grain, paper fiber, and worn edges

If it becomes a generic Buddhist poster:

- reduce literal temple scenery and glowing halos
- make it a layered paper assemblage with cropped sculpture and abstract material traces
- keep text short and imperfect

If it becomes scrapbook clutter:

- remove stickers, lace, Polaroids, excessive flowers, and many decorative scraps
- return to one subject, one secondary lotus/leaf system, and one accent

If it becomes too dark or muddy:

- add cream glyphs, dull gold traces, pale lotus petals, or a small cinnabar seal
- preserve the black/green base while clarifying the subject edge

If it becomes commercial:

- remove large headline hierarchy, CTA, logo placement, product framing, and glossy lighting
- strengthen matte scanned-paper finish and devotional quiet
