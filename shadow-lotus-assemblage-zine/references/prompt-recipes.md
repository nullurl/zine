# Prompt Recipes

Use these recipes when translating a theme into the shadow-lotus assemblage system.

## Edge Statue

Use for serene, reflective, meditative, or memory-heavy themes.

- oversized grayscale Buddha or stone statue face cropped along the left or right edge
- dark green/black background with subtle paper grain
- lotus, vellum, or smoke floats near the cheek, eye, or shoulder
- vertical Chinese text sits beside the face, never as a commercial headline

## Central Shrine

Use for ritual, offering, spring, rain, remembrance, or shrine-like subjects.

- centered layered assemblage with statue fragment, lotus, seals, and torn/vellum panels
- surrounding field remains dark and quiet
- small gold or cinnabar accents make the center readable

## Lotus Orbit

Use for botanical, tropical, river, pond, growth, or dream themes.

- lotus flower or leaf becomes the primary colored material
- statue or sculpture fragment appears as a ghosted grayscale layer behind it
- calligraphy, seals, and smoke create an orbit rather than a frame

## Vellum Scripture

Use for poetry, scripture-like phrases, editorials, or text-forward posters.

- translucent vertical vellum strips carry short calligraphic text
- faded statue face or lotus sits beneath the layers
- keep text partially legible and materially integrated

## Red Panel Ritual

Use for stronger graphic energy, racing-poster influence, sharp emotion, or modern editorial impact.

- one translucent deep red or cinnabar geometric block intersects the dark field
- grayscale statue crop and lotus layer sit above or below the panel
- arrows, circles, or blocks become abstract material marks, not diagram labels

## Diagram Fusion

Use when the user explicitly references the structural diagram images.

- map background to dark paper field
- map material to vellum, leaves, smoke, red panel, or torn paper
- map glyph to vertical calligraphy, seals, microtext, or fragmented letters
- map subject to statue face, lotus, sculpture hand, or shrine fragment
- do not render the diagram words themselves

## Smoke Ribbon

Use for cloud, rain, sea, incense, dream, and invisible-motion themes.

- smoke, ribbon, or thin gold thread is the main path through the poster
- statue and lotus appear as anchored objects inside the flow
- text follows the vertical or curved movement

## Prompt Skeleton

```text
Portrait 4:5 dark devotional assemblage zine poster, black-olive fibrous paper field with smoky ink texture, flat scanned matte surface, visible layered logic of background, translucent material, glyph marks, and one dominant subject.

[MAIN SUBJECT] as an oversized grayscale stone or aged bronze sculptural fragment, [crop and placement], calm and worn, softened by photocopy grain, ink bleed, and paper texture, not glossy or cinematic.

[LAYOUT FAMILY] composition with [LOTUS/LEAF/VELLUM/SMOKE MATERIALS], translucent overlays, torn edges, faint gold thread or smoke ribbon, arranged with controlled negative space and clear hierarchy.

Short vertical Chinese calligraphy "[TITLE]" plus tiny serif microtext or date, small cinnabar seal or dull gold mark as the only warm accent, restrained palette of black, deep green, gray stone, aged cream, and [ACCENT].

Quiet ceremonial editorial mood, mysterious archival paper object, old print defects, dust, soft blur, worn ink; avoid clean infographic labels, commercial headline, temple tourism photo, glossy 3D mockup, neon cyberpunk, cute scrapbook, long text, logos, and watermarks.
```
