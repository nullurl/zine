# Textile Swatch Zine Style Grammar v0.3

Use this file when a request asks to reverse-engineer a reference, merge visual grammars, or correct style drift.

## Reverse-Engineered Reference Grammar

The v0.3 target language is a quiet material-and-label archive:

- vertical bright off-white, pearl gray, or sea-glass mint fibrous paper
- high-key lighting, very low contrast, soft gray-green shadows, no dramatic depth
- rounded cards, pill tabs, ticket panels, film-strip frames, corner brackets, punched holes, dotted rails, circles, arrows, and small check marks
- translucent frosted paper, milky vellum, matte acetate-like slips, and lightly embossed chip edges
- soft mint-gray, pale celadon, pearl white, desaturated sage, and rare darker green-gray accents
- many small independent pieces arranged with disciplined spacing, not one dense scrapbook pile
- thin line rules, dotted guide lines, tiny counters, and almost-text marks instead of readable paragraphs
- shallow contact shadows and paper-lift relief, roughly 1-3 mm deep
- generous white field around the system, with the page still reading as a flat scan or overhead archive

The result should feel like a handmade sticker-sheet, label library, film-frame specimen board, or material notation sheet. It must remain tactile and paper-based, not a digital UI kit.

## Material Archive Layer

Keep the older textile identity alive through one or more of:

- handmade paper chips with deckled or cut edges
- linen, raw silk, cotton, satin, rib knit, felt, or brushed fabric inserts
- translucent tape strips, narrow label rails, punched tabs, and threaded dots
- softly printed texture, scan noise, fiber speckle, and faint watercolor-like stains

When the reference is mostly graphical, use tactile paper and vellum chips as the main material. Do not force heavy fabric swatches if that would break the reference effect.

## Minimal Zine Fusion

Bring from Minimal Zine:

- blank paper is active composition, not leftover space
- one small concentrated attention structure
- restrained, poetic text or no text at all
- one clear chromatic anchor or one preserved pastel palette
- scan noise, paper fibers, old-print softness
- low-to-medium contrast and quiet editorial tone

Replace from Minimal Zine:

- do not use a single isolated tiny object as the whole poster
- do not rely on fragmented letters as the only visual anchor
- do not make the color accent only a tiny registration dot

The swatch or label-board system is the anchor. It should occupy roughly 30%-65% of the page, usually above center or centered, while the remaining paper field stays quiet.

## Physical Construction

Make paper, fabric, and label pieces believable by specifying:

- fibers: coarse wool, boucle yarn, brushed cotton, raw silk, satin, linen, rib knit, mesh, felt, handmade paper, soft leather
- graphic cuts: rounded corners, tag notches, film perforations, stamp scallops, punched holes, corner brackets, pill ends
- edges: frayed selvage, deckled paper, cut felt, lifted fabric corner, loose thread tail, stitched tab, translucent overlap
- surface: pile, loop, rib, weave, sheen, embossing, grain, small wrinkles, soft printed speckle
- depth: 1-3 mm lift, shallow contact shadow, no heavy theatrical shadow
- attachment: tucked under frosted card, pinned with tiny stitch, clipped by a narrow label strip, or taped with matte translucent tape

Avoid vague words like "texture samples" alone. Name the material and its surface behavior.

## Composition Ratios

- Canvas: 2:3 or 3:4 portrait.
- Paper field: 45%-70% visible background.
- Swatch or label system: 30%-55% of the canvas; up to 65% when making a full sticker-sheet board.
- Accent: 1%-8% of the page or one card/chip.
- Text: less than 5% of page; tiny, intentionally placed, and optional.

Default placement: grid starts around 8%-16% from the top, centered horizontally, with a large lower or side paper field.

## Light And Camera

Use flat-lay or orthographic scan language:

- soft diffuse daylight
- matte paper fibers
- high-key exposure
- low contrast with pale gray-green shadows
- shallow contact shadows
- slight print wear, dust, scan noise
- no deep perspective, no macro bokeh, no cinematic spotlight

## Style Drift Corrections

If the output becomes UI:

- replace "cards" with "frosted translucent paper sample slips and label chips"
- add "irregular paper fibers, lifted edges, punched holes, perforated strips, shallow physical contact shadows"
- add "not app cards, not digital UI panels, not a web component library"

If the output becomes retail:

- remove "color chart", "catalog", "product", "brand"
- add "bespoke studio archive, unbranded material ledger, fictional index labels"

If the output becomes too dark:

- specify "bright pearl-white paper, airy daylight, pale gray-green shadows, high-key scanned paper"
- keep dark green-gray only as one label edge, type mark, or small swatch

If the output becomes too scrapbook-like:

- reduce photos, stickers, tickets, handwriting, lace, and decoration
- return to disciplined rows of label chips, tabs, film frames, and material swatches

If material looks flat:

- name the tactile structure of each sample
- add lifted edges, frayed fibers, knit ribs, satin fold highlights, looped yarn, paper embossing, perforations, and translucent overlaps
