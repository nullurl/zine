# Botanical Scrapbook Zine Style Grammar

Use this reference when analyzing user-supplied images, translating a theme into a prompt, or correcting an output that drifted toward a generic mood board.

## 1. Visual Thesis

The page should feel like a personal garden walk reorganized afterward on paper. Natural photographs supply lived evidence; paper fragments, clipped language, and small graphic marks supply reflection. The result is bright, tender, tactile, and editorial.

This is not minimalism through emptiness alone. It is controlled incompleteness: some photos are clipped by the canvas, some sentences stop early, and materials appear in small episodes rather than covering the page.

## 2. Canvas and Paper

- Default to a vertical 2:3 canvas; use 3:5 for a taller phone-poster.
- Use warm white, cloudy ivory, or cool pale gray paper.
- Keep the paper visibly fibrous, lightly mottled, matte, and softly scanned.
- Preserve 35%-65% visible paper. Empty areas should connect the collage zones rather than look accidentally unfinished.
- Use diffuse daylight or a flat scanned-paper treatment. Physical shadows should be shallow and local.

## 3. Attention Geometry

Strong pages use one of these routes:

- half-photo / half-paper split
- loose vertical drift of photographs
- compact center stack with wide surrounding paper
- lower-page binder or folder with open space above
- one physical container centered against a blurred place background
- diagonal photo route connected by type strips or small marks

Use one primary dense zone and at most one secondary zone. Let some elements cross the zone boundary, but do not scatter equal-weight objects everywhere.

## 4. Photograph Grammar

Use three to seven photographs from one credible memory:

- one anchor image with clear place or gesture
- two to four close observations
- zero to two imperfect transitional frames

Useful anchors:

- sunlit canopy
- flowering shrub
- hand holding a flower, leaf, book, or small found object
- open notebook on grass
- forest or garden path
- pond or rain reflection

Useful supports:

- leaves against light
- white flowers against dark foliage
- bark, grass, shadow, water, stones, or an edge of clothing
- a blurred moving view
- a small personal object in the same environment

Mix borderless crops, Polaroids, narrow vertical strips, framed prints, or contact sheets. Do not use all formats equally. Keep season, weather, plant species, daylight direction, camera response, and color grade believable across the set.

Photographs should feel personal:

- slight exposure inconsistency is welcome
- one frame may be soft or motion blurred
- compositions may be casual
- avoid polished stock-photo perfection

## 5. Material Grammar

Choose one dominant support system:

- lined notebook or index paper
- kraft folder or ledger
- Polaroid stack
- contact strip
- open ring album
- shallow silver keepsake tin
- clipped newspaper or book strips

Add no more than two material accents:

- paper clip
- lace edge
- translucent tape
- ribbon
- tag
- receipt or barcode
- torn color swatch
- pressed botanical fragment

Materials should overlap with plausible physical order. Keep edges, staples, clips, and tape visible enough to read, but avoid deep 3D staging.

## 6. Typography Grammar

Use text as physical matter:

- serif or typewriter text printed on narrow beige strips
- rough cut-paper letters in mixed sizes
- short handwritten note on translucent or colored paper
- tiny index, date, or field-label text

Use at most three voices and keep one dominant. Good text behavior includes stepped strips, staggered words, clipped margins, small misregistration, and slightly imperfect baselines.

Text limits:

- title: 2-7 words
- sentence: at most 8-12 words
- note: one or two short handwritten lines
- metadata: tiny and optional

Invent original text unless the user supplies exact wording. Do not repeat quotations, lyrics, usernames, signatures, or personal phrases extracted only from references.

## 7. Graphic Marks

Choose one mark family:

- butter-yellow four-point glints
- olive indexing squares
- cyan outlined drops or loops
- black arrows or halftone cutouts
- sparse music notation
- simple quotation-mark block
- one large numeral sequence

Use two to seven marks total depending on scale. They should explain sunlight, rain, route, sound, sequence, or attention. Decorative marks without narrative function create style drift.

## 8. Color Logic

The base palette comes from the photographs:

- leaf and moss greens
- flower white
- sunlit yellow-green
- bark and paper neutrals
- occasional water cyan

Choose one added accent occupying about 1%-5% of the page. Keep other inserted materials neutral. Natural images may be richly green, but avoid nuclear saturation, muddy black-green, or a single dark teal wash across the page.

Reliable pairings:

- leaf green + butter yellow
- hydrangea white + black + cyan
- fern green + pale olive
- spring green + cobalt cut letters
- rainy garden green + vermilion route arrows

## 9. Texture and Reproduction

Combine:

- visible paper tooth
- subtle scan noise
- soft film grain
- mild bloom in sunlit leaves
- shallow tape or photo-edge shadows
- restrained halftone or photocopy wear on graphic elements
- tiny alignment errors between printed layers

Do not apply the same blur or grain strength to everything. The page needs material separation: fibrous paper, photographic emulsion, printed type, and handwritten ink should read differently.

## 10. Anti-Identity

Reject outputs that read as:

- a social media template
- a UI card grid
- an e-commerce flat lay
- a brand mood board
- a maximal sticker scrapbook
- a wedding invitation
- a school craft project
- a dark cinematic poster
- a polished fashion editorial
- a full-bleed nature photograph with text on top

Also reject repeated images, random quotations, illegible pseudo-paragraphs, heavy drop shadows, too many artifact types, or no meaningful visible paper.

## 11. Reference Extraction Checklist

For each new reference set, record:

1. canvas ratio and paper tone
2. visible-paper percentage
3. primary attention zone
4. photo count and crop types
5. anchor subject and memory thread
6. dominant support material
7. type voices and amount of text
8. accent color and mark family
9. lighting and reproduction texture
10. three most important avoids

Translate these observations into prompt constraints. Do not include source paths, analysis language, or copied reference text in the final image prompt.
