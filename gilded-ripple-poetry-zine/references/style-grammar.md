# Style Grammar

Use this file when analyzing a reference, correcting a drifted result, or strengthening the prompt.

## Visual DNA

- The poster is an aged paper object first, then an illustrated water poem.
- The page is decorative but ordered: title, subtitle, central panel, Chinese title, rules, labels, and metadata all align to a deliberate grid.
- The central image panel should look printed, not photographed: risograph grain, halftone, old lithograph softness, imperfect ink registration, and fibrous paper absorption.
- The poster should feel literary and collectible, like a small-press water-poetry broadside or vintage zine cover.

## Composition

- Canvas: vertical 3:4, 4:5, or 3:5. Prefer `1024x1536` for generation.
- Margins: visible cream paper margin on all sides; no full bleed.
- Central panel: 35%-55% of height, usually centered. It can sit slightly lower if the title is large.
- Top type: large ornate English title occupies 10%-20% of height.
- Subtitle band: bold all-caps serif, usually 1-2 lines, placed under title, inside a rule band, or under the panel.
- Chinese type: one vertical column along the left margin, or a bottom-right title block. Keep it short.
- Ornament: dotted rules, small star/flower marks, oval labels, tiny page numbers, small seals, registration crosses, and microtext.

## Subject Translation

Translate abstract themes into a water-adjacent poetic image:

- memory -> ripples crossing an old pond, reflected title marks
- cafe -> rainy window reflection, coffee steam becoming small waves
- spring -> pond flowers, green reeds, light rain dots, small birds
- tropical plants -> wet leaves beside blue-green pool water, botanical margin marks
- fish-water-cloud-sea -> fish silhouettes swimming through cloud-like sea bands
- rain -> blue puddle panel, dotted rain rules, wet paper bloom
- dusk -> gold sun reflection on river or coast, warm orange ink on water
- island -> pale coast panel, little boat, shell marks, turquoise water

## Typography

- English title: calligraphic, engraved, vintage script, or ornamental serif. It can be imperfect, but must read as a title.
- Subtitle: all-caps condensed serif or slab serif. Keep it short: 2-6 words.
- Chinese title: short and prominent enough to be seen, but not the only focal point.
- Microtext: can be semi-legible. Ask for metadata-like rows, tiny labels, and editorial marks, not paragraphs.
- Avoid exact reproduction of reference words unless the user supplies them as intended image text.

## Color

- Paper: cream, off-white, ivory, tea-stained, pale oatmeal.
- Type: sage, olive green, deep moss, black-brown, faded charcoal.
- Water: aqua, cyan-blue, teal, sea blue, pale ultramarine.
- Accent: golden yellow, ochre, marigold, orange, or warm coral. Use one warm glimmer accent, not a rainbow palette.
- If the result is too dark, strengthen with `bright cream paper`, `sunlit aqua water`, `clear golden glimmer`, and `medium-light ink contrast`.

## Texture

Use concrete print language:

- risograph grain
- lithograph speckle
- letterpress ink bleed
- offset misregistration
- uneven paper fibers
- scan dust
- rubbed ink edges
- yellowed paper mottling
- soft panel-edge wear

## Drift Corrections

- Too modern: add `old small-press literary poster`, `aged paper`, `engraved title`, `letterpress rules`, `no modern branding`.
- Too scrapbook: add `strict editorial grid`, `no stickers`, `no Polaroids`, `no random stationery`.
- Too minimal: add `ornamental title, serif subtitle band, vertical Chinese title, dotted rules, oval label`.
- Too dark: add `bright cream page`, `clear daylight`, `aqua water`, `warm gold highlight`, `no low-key lighting`.
- Too much text: add `short title only, microtext as decorative marks, no paragraphs`.
- Missing water: add `central rectangular blue-green water illustration panel occupying half the page`.
