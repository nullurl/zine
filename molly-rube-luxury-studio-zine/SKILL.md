# Molly Rube Luxury Studio Zine

Turn the user's content into both:

1. a final image-generation prompt, and
2. a generated raster image made from that prompt.

## Source Grammar

Use this visual identity:

- vertical 4:5 or square editorial brand-system image; 3:5 allowed for phone/UI and poster variants
- restrained luxury content-studio world: espresso brown, black, ivory, warm grey, soft cream, silver, muted glass
- objects: dark embossed business cards, perfume bottle, hairbrush, lipstick, soap embossing, black pointed shoes, leather pouch, silver tray, printed portraits, phone portfolio screen
- layouts: stacked printed photos, pinned 3x3 moodboard, dark business-card close-up, phone on velvet, off-white typography poster, blurred vertical wordmark construction grid
- typography: uppercase sans/serif wordmark, tiny serif contact info, role labels, roman numerals, restrained editorial copy
- materials: leather, velvet, heavy paper stock, blind embossing, letterpress, off-white card, glossy black product, silver metal tray, soft studio reflection
- mood: boutique luxury content agency, editorial photography, social-first portfolio, intimate creative director identity, quiet restraint
- visual finish: dim natural light, soft grain, matte shadows, shallow focus, slightly aged print, no colorful decoration

The image should feel like a disciplined luxury identity presentation, not a generic logo mockup or a loud marketing ad.

## Mode Policy

Use **Standard Mode** for all generation. Use the Standard Mode Prompt Compiler below to convert the user's content into compact, imageable prompts.

Use prompt-only output only when the user explicitly asks for prompt-only.

## Standard Mode Prompt Compiler

Default generation should compile only visible image instructions.

### First-Principles Fields

Every prompt must answer these rendering questions in this order:

1. **Canvas:** What is the frame?
   - square, 4:5, or vertical 3:5 editorial brand-system poster; no app UI unless the recipe is phone portfolio.

2. **Brand Subject:** What is being branded?
   - convert the user's content into a boutique content studio, creative director identity, luxury personal brand, editorial agency, beauty studio, photography portfolio, or product-styling practice.

3. **Core Artifact:** What carries attention?
   - choose one: embossed business cards, pinned moodboard grid, phone portfolio screen, Polaroid stack, off-white manifesto poster, luxury still life, wordmark construction sheet, or product-and-card arrangement.

4. **Still-Life Objects:** What objects make the brand world specific?
   - choose two to five: perfume bottle, hairbrush, lipstick, leather pouch, black shoes, soap bar, silver tray, printed portrait, stack of cards, spiral notebook, cosmetic jar, folded paper.

5. **Material Treatment:** What makes it luxury and tactile?
   - blind embossing, letterpress ink, matte brown card stock, leather grain, velvet nap, glossy black product, silver tray reflection, off-white paper fibers, pinned photo paper, soft shadows.

6. **Typography System:** How does text behave?
   - uppercase wordmark; tiny serif role/contact details; sparse numbered menu lines; short studio description; roman numerals; construction grid labels. Keep text short and elegant.

7. **Color Logic:** What palette controls the image?
   - espresso brown, black, ivory, warm grey, cream paper, silver highlight. Avoid strong accent colors; tiny warm glass or skin tone is allowed.

8. **Composition Structure:** How are artifacts arranged?
   - choose centered hero card, diagonal card over photo stack, 3x3 pinned grid, phone at oblique angle on velvet, large negative-space poster, scattered Polaroids on black cloth, or grid-based wordmark close-up.

9. **Reproduction Texture:** What makes it feel photographed or printed?
   - film grain, soft focus, dust, paper texture, low contrast, dim ambient light, subtle blur, matte surface scuffs, aged editorial scan.

10. **Emotional Temperature:** What should the viewer feel first?
   - restraint, quiet wealth, editorial taste, creative intimacy, archived studio world, private luxury, deliberate minimalism.

11. **Hard Avoids:** What must not appear?
   - neon, glossy tech UI, bright fashion ad, influencer flat-lay clutter, generic logo mockup, colorful stationery, cute stickers, loud gradients, plastic product shoot, busy text.

### Standard Prompt Shape

Write the final Standard Mode prompt as four compact paragraphs:

1. canvas + brand subject + core artifact + composition
2. still-life objects + material treatment + lighting
3. typography + palette + reproduction texture
4. mood + text policy + avoid-list

Describe the final image only. Do not mention the reference images or this skill.

## Workflow

1. Parse the user's content.
   - Identify the brand name, role, service, product category, contact details, and mood.
   - If no brand name is supplied, invent a short luxury studio name. Avoid famous brand names.
   - If exact text is supplied, use it sparingly; image models may distort small text.

2. Select a variation recipe.
   - Choose layout, core artifact, still-life objects, typography mode, material mode, and palette from the Variation Engine.
   - For batches, use different layout families so outputs do not repeat the same card stack.

3. Write the final image prompt.
   - Use the Standard Prompt Shape.
   - Specify brand artifact placement, object roles, material surface, lighting, palette, and text hierarchy.
   - Keep typography short: one wordmark, a role line, a tiny contact line, or a short studio sentence.
   - If the recipe is phone portfolio, describe a minimal dark website screen but avoid generic app UI.

4. Generate the image.
   - Use the available image generation capability by default.
   - Do not stop after prompt-only unless the user asks for prompt-only.
   - If the result becomes a generic logo mockup, cluttered influencer flatlay, or bright tech UI, tighten the prompt and regenerate once with stronger `dark luxury editorial identity`, `blind embossing`, `espresso brown`, `serif microtype`, and `restrained studio archive` wording.

5. Return the image and prompt.

## Negative Constraints

Always avoid:

- bright neon, cyberpunk, colorful gradients, playful stickers
- generic logo mockup on a blank card with no brand world
- tech startup UI, glossy app design, dashboard interface
- loud fashion campaign, influencer clutter, maximal flat-lay
- plastic product photography, oversaturated beauty ad
- cute stationery, scrapbook tape, craft collage
- famous brand names or trademarked luxury labels
- excessive text blocks, menu-heavy layouts, unreadable tiny paragraphs
- 3D render look, perfect vector-only presentation
- sterile white mockup without leather/paper/product texture

## Quality Gate

Before finalizing, check:

- Did the run use the Standard Mode Prompt Compiler?
- Did the run choose a recipe across layout, artifact, objects, typography, material, and palette?
- Does the image read as a luxury studio identity system rather than a generic logo mockup?
- Are espresso brown, black, ivory, cream, warm grey, or silver the dominant colors?
- Are tactile materials visible: leather, velvet, heavy card stock, blind embossing, paper, glass, metal, or photo prints?
- Is there a clear brand artifact such as business card, moodboard, phone portfolio, poster, Polaroid stack, or wordmark grid?
- Is typography restrained and editorial, not a big advertising headline?
- Does the composition avoid bright tech UI, neon, cute stationery, cluttered influencer flatlay, and generic mockup aesthetics?
- Did you actually generate the image?
