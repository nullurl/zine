# Style Grammar

Use this file when analyzing a reference, correcting drift, or tightening a prompt.

## Visual DNA

- The page is mostly empty paper, like a diagram waiting for one thought.
- A small analytical object sits inside a sparse system of axes, arrows, coordinates, or measurement marks.
- The diagram is not a dashboard or infographic. It is a poetic scientific plate.
- The page reads as archival, contemplative, and lightly technical.

## Composition

- Canvas: vertical 3:4, 4:5, or 3:5.
- Background: cream, warm white, off-white, or pale gray paper.
- Empty field: 80%-95% of the frame.
- Main cluster: 5%-20% of the canvas, usually centered or slightly low.
- Lines: thin, precise, faint, and deliberate.
- Labels: small and sparse, placed only where they support the central idea.

## Anchor Types

Use one anchor only:

- compass, axis, or crosshair
- bird sequence
- head silhouette or profile
- object specimen
- timeline or horizon line
- flow-field or arrow system
- split study with two forms

The anchor should feel like a conceptual image, not a scene.

## Line Language

- Use pencil-gray, charcoal, faded black, blue ink, red ink, or gold accent lines.
- Keep line weight thin and controlled.
- Let arrows curve, drift, or branch lightly.
- Use tick marks, tiny circles, and crosshairs only when they help the structure.
- Avoid heavy borders, boxes, or chart frames.

## Typography

- Main text: small serif, typewriter, monospaced, or fine printed labels.
- Poetic note: one or two short Chinese lines.
- Optional English note: one short phrase or technical subtitle.
- Labels: tiny axis names, measurement hints, or specimen tags.
- Do not build a full paragraph layout.

## Color

- Paper: cream, warm white, ivory, pale gray, tea-stain.
- Base marks: graphite, charcoal, soft black.
- Accent: one precise hue only, usually blue, red, magenta, gold, or cyan.
- Accent may appear as one line, one point, one arrow, one subject, or one highlight region.
- If the result feels flat, enlarge the accent slightly rather than adding more colors.

## Texture

Use concrete print language:

- scanned paper
- fine grain
- slight dust
- pencil smudge
- ink fade
- soft registration drift
- matte absorbent paper
- faint fold or handling wear

## Drift Corrections

- Too empty: add one analytical anchor, a few coordinates, and one poetic note.
- Too busy: remove most labels and keep only one system of lines.
- Too much like an infographic: add `poetic archival diagram`, `sparse scientific plate`, `matte paper`.
- Too dark: add `bright cream paper`, `soft daylight`, `low-contrast reproduction`.
- Too colorful: reduce to one accent hue.
- Too literal scene: turn objects into silhouettes, studies, or field notes.
