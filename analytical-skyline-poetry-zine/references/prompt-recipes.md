# Prompt Recipes

Use one recipe per image. Keep the paper field dominant.

## Recipe Fields

Before writing the final prompt, choose:

- Layout family
- Anchor type
- Accent color
- Label density
- Poetic note placement
- Line style

## Layout Families

### central-axis

Use for orientation, self-positioning, origin, or meditation themes.

- One small anchor at the center of axes or crosshairs.
- Optional tiny coordinates or tick marks.
- Large blank paper around it.

Prompt phrase:
`a small central figure or object sitting on a thin crosshair system inside a vast cream paper field, with faint coordinates and one poetic note below`

### horizon-line

Use for time, distance, migration, sky, or quiet procession.

- One thin horizontal line or horizon band.
- Small repeated marks or figures distributed along it.
- Major empty field above and below.

Prompt phrase:
`a sparse horizon-line diagram across a large off-white paper field, with tiny figures, labels, and one small accent mark`

### flow-field

Use for breath, thought, wind, force, motion, or uncertainty.

- One small anchor with arrows or curves radiating outward.
- Keep the field airy, not crowded.
- Let the arrows remain light and analytical.

Prompt phrase:
`a tiny anchor at the center of a pale paper flow-field, with thin arrows and curves drifting outward like a scientific-poetic field study`

### split-study

Use for contrast, dialogue, relation, before/after, or paired states.

- Two related forms placed with a measured gap.
- Add one shared axis or connective line.
- Keep text sparse.

Prompt phrase:
`a split analytical study of two related forms on a cream paper field, connected by a fine line and annotated with short poetic labels`

### head-map

Use for memory, mind, language, identity, or internal landscape.

- Head profile or silhouette filled with a small map of marks, labels, or cloud-like diagrams.
- Leave the rest of the page open.

Prompt phrase:
`a pale head-map study on a wide blank paper field, with one accent region, thin labels, and a short Chinese poetic note`

### specimen-array

Use for object studies, botanical-technical hybrids, or archival notes.

- One primary specimen plus a few tiny supporting insets.
- Keep insets small and subordinate.
- Avoid turning it into a catalog page.

Prompt phrase:
`one small specimen study with a few tiny inset labels on a quiet cream paper page, analytical but poetic`

## Text Strategy

- Poetic note: one or two Chinese lines.
- English note: optional, short, and technical or reflective.
- Labels: tiny and mostly functional.
- If the user supplies a phrase, place it where the poetic note would go.

## Prompt Skeleton

Use this structure:

```text
Vertical 4:5 sparse poster on cream paper, 80-95 percent empty field, matte scan feel, no border and no mockup.

One small analytical [anchor type] in the [center / lower-middle / slightly off-center], with thin [axes / arrows / coordinates / curves / tick marks], occupying only a small cluster of the page, delicate linework, faint graphite or ink trace.

Typography: tiny serif or monospaced labels, one short Chinese poetic note near the lower third, optional short English subtitle, one precise accent color [blue / red / magenta / gold / cyan] used only on the main line, point, or subject.

Texture: scanned paper, light grain, soft wear, faint dust, low contrast, restrained archival plate feel, no heavy framing.

Mood: quiet analytical poetry, scientific but meditative, minimal and reflective. Avoid infographic overload, dashboard styling, dense charts, glossy render, neon, cartoon, or full-scene illustration.
```

## Variation Guidance

For multiple outputs, vary only one or two axes:

- anchor type
- accent color
- axis geometry
- text placement
- label density

Keep the work within a sparse poetic diagram language.
