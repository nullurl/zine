# Warm Clay Brand Editorial Zine

Turn the user's content into both:

1. a final image-generation prompt, and
2. a generated raster image made from that prompt.

## Source Grammar

Use this visual grammar as the style core:

- **Surface:** warm cream, rice paper, recycled paper, or aged poster paper with a thick taupe outer margin and visible fibers, stains, folds, and print wear.
- **Palette:** clay brown, burnt umber, walnut, tea stain, warm beige, muted ochre, soft honey yellow, faded black, and occasional muted blue or straw-yellow icon color.
- **Brand language:** quiet Chinese/English brand editorial identity, restaurant or studio tone, restrained promotional content, natural materials, nourishment, warmth, patience, healing, opening event, record of place.
- **Typography:** vertical Chinese title, large clay-brown Chinese logotype, serif English headline, small bilingual body copy, handwritten Chinese phrase or signature, stamp-like labels, and rough letterpress registration.
- **Image anchors:** hanging lamp over table, bowl or plate of food, grocery bag with black food icons, product-card dessert photo, wood-grain triptych, framed pictograms, architectural line drawing, paper cutout bird/fish/hand, or natural material swatches.
- **Composition:** large negative space, centered or offset brand cluster, small text blocks, one or two focal objects, and generous breathing room.
- **Mood:** warm, grounded, handmade, calm, nourishing, female-friendly, natural studio, slow dining, wood and clay materiality.

## Mode Policy

Use **Standard Mode** for all generation. Compile only the visual details that should become pixels. Do not include analysis prose, source filenames, or process notes inside the final image prompt.

## Standard Mode Prompt Compiler

Every prompt must describe a single finished poster image, not a logo sheet, website UI, or generic brand board.

### First-Principles Fields

1. **Canvas and Paper**
   - Vertical 3:5 poster by default, with warm taupe margin around a cream inner sheet.
   - Full-frame flat paper view or softly scanned poster appearance.
   - 55%-80% quiet paper negative space.
   - Add rice-paper fibers, faint wrinkles, tea stains, letterpress softness, and slightly uneven edges.

2. **Brand Premise**
   - Convert the user's content into one warm brand or event premise: restaurant, cafe, studio, nature workshop, product launch, exhibition, dining therapy, wellness object, or opening poster.
   - Choose one short brand phrase or title in Chinese or English.
   - Keep the premise subtle and editorial, not sales-heavy.

3. **Primary Image Anchor**
   - Use one central imageable anchor or one structured small group.
   - Suitable anchors include: pendant lamp above a table, plate of round desserts, warm grocery bag with food silhouettes, wood-grain macro triptych, three framed pictograms, architecture line drawing, small product poster, hand/bird/fish cutout, or natural material panel.
   - Render anchors as flat paper illustration, muted photo crop, rough print block, framed mini print, or translucent paper cutout.

4. **Typography System**
   - Mix vertical Chinese characters, serif English headings, small bilingual body copy, and one loose handwritten Chinese phrase.
   - Allow one large hand-drawn signature or phrase across the lower middle.
   - Use tiny captions for time, address, opening date, special event, studio tagline, or material notes.
   - Text may be partly imperfect or semi-legible, but key short phrases should be readable.

5. **Warm Color Logic**
   - Use clay brown or walnut as the dominant ink color.
   - Use cream and tea-stain paper as the base.
   - Add only one or two small muted accents such as honey yellow lamp glow, faded blue pictogram, straw-yellow food shape, or black icon silhouettes.
   - Avoid high-chroma digital color; the poster should feel printed with natural pigments.

6. **Editorial Information Blocks**
   - When event-like, include time/address/opening/date blocks, black reverse label strips, bullet list, and thin architectural line drawing.
   - When brand-like, include slogan, small body paragraph, product card, repeated brand characters, and signature line.
   - When material-like, include three image panels or framed symbols aligned in a row.

7. **Reproduction Texture**
   - Paper scan, risograph grain, letterpress bleed, faded ink, soft shadow from paper folds, low contrast, and tactile old-poster wear.
   - Keep the whole image flat and composed; avoid glossy product photography.

8. **Emotional Temperature**
   - Warm, healthy, patient, sincere, handmade, natural, relaxed, inclusive, nostalgic, calm, quietly premium.
   - The viewer should feel a small local brand with care and warmth before reading the details.

9. **Hard Avoids**
   - Avoid glossy corporate brand deck, modern SaaS layout, neon color, luxury fashion campaign, food photography spread, crowded menu, clean vector infographic, 3D render, plastic packaging mockup, hard shadows, high-contrast black-white poster, cute cartoon overload, or commercial CTA-heavy ad.

### Standard Prompt Shape

Write the final Standard Mode prompt as four compact paragraphs:

1. canvas, paper surface, margin, negative space, overall palette
2. brand premise and primary image anchor with material treatment
3. typography system, bilingual text blocks, calligraphy/signature, event or brand details
4. print texture, mood, lighting, and avoid-list

Prefer concrete visible instructions over abstract style words. If the user supplies no text, invent one short Chinese title, one English subtitle, and one small slogan.

## Variation Engine

Before writing the prompt, choose one option from each axis. For multiple images, vary at least three axes per image.

## Workflow

1. Determine mode.
   - Use Standard Mode.

2. Parse the user's content.
   - Identify the core brand, event, material, mood, exact text if supplied, and any reference image role.
   - For complex content, extract one brand premise and one imageable anchor rather than illustrating every idea.
   - If no text is supplied, invent a short Chinese title, English subtitle, and tiny slogan that match the mood.

3. Select a variation recipe.
   - Pick layout, anchor type, typography mode, texture mode, color mode, and mood.
   - Do not overfill the poster. Keep enough paper space for a quiet editorial brand feeling.
   - If recent outputs used the same food or bag structure, switch to wood, frame, lamp, or event-ledger grammar.

4. Write the final image prompt.
   - Use the four-paragraph Standard Prompt Shape.
   - State exact placements: where the title sits, where the anchor sits, how large the text cluster is, and how much paper remains empty.
   - Keep exact in-image text short; use microtext visually for longer body copy.

5. Generate the image.
   - Use the built-in image generation capability by default.
   - Do not stop after prompt-only unless the user explicitly asks for prompt-only.
   - If the result becomes glossy, corporate, menu-like, or too colorful, tighten the aged paper, clay ink, natural pigment, and sparse editorial constraints and regenerate once.

6. Return the image and prompt.

## Negative Constraints

Always avoid:

- glossy corporate brand board or modern pitch-deck layout
- commercial CTA-heavy ad, ecommerce product mockup, luxury fashion campaign
- dense restaurant menu, full food photography spread, appetizing macro food realism
- clean vector infographic, SaaS UI, web page, app interface
- neon, cyberpunk, high-chroma gradients, plastic packaging shine
- 3D render, CGI, cinematic spotlights, hard shadows, dramatic depth of field
- cute cartoon overload, anime style, maximal scrapbook
- perfect digital typography, long crisp paragraphs, logo-only sheet
- cold black-white minimalism without warm paper or clay-brown identity

## Quality Gate

Before finalizing, check:

- Did the prompt create a warm cream or rice-paper poster with taupe margin or aged paper surface?
- Does 55%-80% of the image remain quiet paper space?
- Is clay brown, walnut, tea stain, ochre, or warm beige the main visual palette?
- Is there one clear brand or event premise rather than a generic collage?
- Is the image anchor one of the style's material anchors: lamp/table, food plate, bag, product card, wood panels, framed pictograms, architecture line, or paper cutout?
- Are vertical Chinese, English serif text, small bilingual copy, handwritten Chinese, or signature marks included?
- If event-like, are time/address/opening/date blocks or bullet-ledger details present?
- Does the texture read as paper scan, letterpress, risograph, woodcut, aged poster, or rice fiber?
- Does the mood feel handmade, warm, natural, nourishing, and quietly editorial?
- Did the prompt avoid glossy corporate, UI, ecommerce, neon, 3D, menu clutter, and hard commercial aesthetics?
- Did you actually generate the image?
