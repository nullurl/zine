# Style Grammar

## Reference Deconstruction

The reference set combines experimental event flyers, album press sheets, type specimens, calendars, and translucent editorial posters. The surfaces are pale and degraded; images are present as ghosts rather than as crisp subjects.

### Stable Features

- portrait poster artboard presented against black in some screenshots, but black is not part of the default poster
- fog white, gray, silver, pale blue, blush, or low-saturation cyan field
- one blurred eye, figure, cloud, tree, bird, or crowd image
- low-opacity photo, toner bloom, halftone, or frosted crop
- typography concentrated at the top or bottom
- bold grotesk mixed with script, serif, custom glyphs, or mono microtext
- small dates, route curves, calendars, tracklists, weight tables, or vertical labels
- one tiny pink, coral, rust, cyan, or cobalt signal
- scan noise, photocopy grain, paper abrasion, low-resolution raster, and transparency

## Attention Geometry

The page usually contains three zones:

1. information zone at top or bottom
2. large atmospheric center field
3. one image or diagram anchor crossing a zone boundary

Keep the center empty enough to make the ghost image perceptible. Do not fill the whole page with text.

## Type Behavior

- text may repeat with reduced opacity to form a texture
- one word may be stretched, deformed, or handwritten across the top
- microtext can run vertically along an edge
- a title may sit over a translucent rectangle or image strip
- dates and months may trace a circular or curved path
- serif and sans can overlap only when one remains clearly primary

## Image Behavior

- blurred photographs are embedded in paper, not floating with frames
- eye/body/hand crops can be softened until they read as sensory evidence
- trees, clouds, birds, and crowd strips can act as memory traces
- one spectral subject is enough
- high opacity should be reserved for a small silhouette, title, or signal mark

## Minimal Zine Fusion

Keep the original Skill's quiet canvas, one image anchor, one signal color, sparse print defects, and editorial distance. Expand microtext into a controlled program layer and replace the tiny paper specimen with a low-opacity subject field.

## Failure Diagnosis

### Looks blank rather than atmospheric

Increase one subject edge, one type weight, or one signal mark. Do not fill the page with more elements.

### Looks like a nightclub flyer

Remove neon, chrome, black background, bright gradient, condensed headline stack, and dense sponsor rows. Restore pale field, low opacity, toner grain, and one quiet information zone.

### Looks like random type experiments

Assign each type layer a job: title, expressive word, program copy, or microtext. Delete layers without a job.

### Ghost image disappears

Raise local contrast around one edge or silhouette and keep the rest translucent. Avoid sharpening the whole photograph.

### Looks too digitally clean

Add paper tooth, scan streak, toner dropout, xerox bloom, low-resolution raster, and slight offset misregistration.
