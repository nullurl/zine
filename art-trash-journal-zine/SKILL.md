# Art Trash Journal Zine

Turn the user's theme, mood, memory, or reference set into:

1. a final image-generation prompt, and
2. a finished raster image of an art-trash journal collage.

Fuse Minimal Zine paper discipline with reclaimed, low-value, overloaded craft material: old newspaper, painted board, moss fiber, glue shine, beads, scraps, dried flowers, branches, miniature objects, torn labels, and imperfect handmade assembly.

## Reference Routing

- Treat supplied images as visual-grammar or material references unless the user explicitly requests a literal edit.
- Analyze local references locally: extract base paper, collage density, waste materials, dominant color, object scale, edge treatment, type remnants, glue/shadow texture, and blank-space share.
- Do not reproduce visible brands, exact headlines, addresses, logos, watermarks, signatures, or distinctive copied text from references.
- Do not upload private local references to an untrusted third-party endpoint. The bundled server fallback is text-only.
- Read references/style-grammar.md when reverse-engineering a reference or correcting style drift.
- Read references/prompt-recipes.md when choosing a layout, material set, title, or batch variation.

## Core Identity

Preserve these signals:

- Portrait 2:3, 3:4, or tall 3:5 paper composition photographed or scanned from above.
- Aged newspaper, book page, ledger paper, cardboard, kraft paper, or stained off-white paper as the base.
- One irregular handmade collage zone, usually occupying 20%-50% of the canvas, with visible paper around it.
- Reclaimed materials: torn magazine scraps, colored card, foil bits, cheap plastic beads, pearls, ticket stubs, labels, wrappers, thread, glue, tape, dried petals, moss fiber, twigs, gauze, mesh, pins, or small thrift-store miniatures.
- One dense tactile anchor: a mossy patch, painted scrap-board island, altered-book window, found-object cluster, or junk altar.
- Short text only: one tiny title, torn label, stamped word, scribbled note, date mark, or half-buried newspaper fragments.
- Imperfect assembly: skewed edges, glue puddles, dust, frayed string, bent paper, lifted corners, mismatched scale, shallow shadows.
- One dominant color family plus one visible accent; green is allowed for moss/trash-garden themes but must not become a polished nature poster.
- Handmade art-room mood: rescued scraps made poetic, not clean stationery, not product styling.

## Fusion With Minimal Zine

Carry forward:

- paper as an active field
- strong empty-space discipline
- one clear attention route
- sparse typography and short readable words
- one chromatic anchor that stays visible at thumbnail size
- flat scan view, old-print defects, paper fibers, and low-to-medium contrast

Break from Minimal Zine deliberately:

- Permit tactile clutter inside the chosen collage zone.
- Let glue, beads, fake flowers, plastic, moss, and scraps be visibly cheap.
- Allow mixed-media relief and awkward handmade scale.
- Keep the chaos local; the whole page must not become a full-bleed mess unless the user explicitly asks for maximum overload.

## Layout Engine

Choose one family before compiling:

- trash-island: one irregular painted or torn board floats on old newspaper, loaded with moss, beads, scraps, and tiny objects.
- altered-book-window: an old book or newspaper base has a torn window into a miniature scene built from waste materials.
- junk-altar: a compact shrine-like cluster of discarded objects, labels, flowers, and beads sits in a large paper field.
- corner-spill: material debris spills from one corner into empty aged paper with scattered type fragments.
- specimen-trash: one reclaimed object or scrap cluster is treated like an archival specimen with tiny labels and evidence marks.
- moss-diorama-page: moss, twigs, beads, toy-like ladders, tiny cages, bottles, and fabric flowers form a shallow handmade diorama on paper.
- type-and-debris: rough type, newspaper columns, and torn lettering are the anchor, with small trash objects embedded around it.

Use one layout family only. Do not combine every object type in every image.

## Material Engine

Translate the user's theme into reclaimed materials:

- place: use local waste cues, map scraps, tickets, wrappers, paper color, and small object metaphors.
- season: choose plant decay, paper stain, thread color, and light temperature.
- mood: choose mess quality: tender, grimy, magical, school-craft, attic, rainy, seaside, or garden.
- object: turn it into a found-object shrine, paper specimen, or altered-book window.
- abstract phrase: invent a small title and a material metaphor, not a literal infographic.

Name specific surfaces. Use "glue shine", "moss fiber", "cracked acrylic paint", "cheap plastic pearl", "bent cardboard", "dusty newspaper ink", "frayed thread", "dried petal", "oxidized staple", or "torn label" instead of generic "mixed media".

## Typography

- Invent one short title of 2-6 words when the user supplies no exact text.
- Use typewriter, old newspaper fragments, rubber-stamp letters, crude marker, receipt type, or tiny handwriting.
- Let some text be partial or semi-legible, but keep the main title short and original.
- Do not ask the image model to render long paragraphs, copied article text, brand packaging, social handles, or exact reference-image text.
- Chinese titles are allowed when the user asks in Chinese; keep them very short.

## Color Engine

Start from aged paper, tan newsprint, cardboard brown, smoke gray, or off-white. Choose one dominant material color and one accent:

- moss green with glassy cyan beads for trash-garden themes
- acidic green with tomato-red scraps for punk art-room tension
- cobalt paint with tarnished foil for urban or rainy themes
- saffron paper with black ink for bookish altered pages
- pale pink plastic pearls with gray newspaper for tender memory
- bottle green with milky white glue shine for miniature dioramas

The dominant color can occupy 10%-35% of the page inside the collage zone. The accent should occupy 1%-6%. Avoid rainbow craft chaos unless the user explicitly asks for maximal trash overload.

## Prompt Compiler

Write the final prompt as six compact paragraphs:

1. Canvas, base paper, ratio, visible blank-space share, scan or flat-lay lighting.
2. Layout family, collage-zone size/location, edge shape, and reading route.
3. Reclaimed material anchor: exact scraps, trash objects, moss/fiber/glue/beads, object scale, physical depth.
4. Typography: exact short title, label fragments, type materials, and text limits.
5. Color, paper texture, print wear, glue shine, dust, shadows, and handmade imperfections.
6. Mood and hard avoids: no brands, UI, retail craft kit, glossy mockup, cute sticker sheet, full-bleed polished scene, or copied text.

Compile only visible renderable details. Never mention source paths, reverse-engineering, or reference-image analysis in the final prompt.

## Generation

- Generate the image by default; stop at prompt-only only when explicitly requested.
- Prefer the built-in image generation capability.
- When built-in generation is unavailable and server fallback has already been approved, run:

    python3 scripts/server_image_gen.py \
      --prompt-file output/imagegen/<slug>.prompt.txt \
      --out output/imagegen/<slug>.png \
      --size 1024x1536 \
      --quality high

- The fallback reads provider configuration and OPENAI_API_KEY from the environment or Codex config, calls the OpenAI-compatible image endpoint, requests b64_json, and writes the decoded image locally. Never print or hard-code secrets.
- Store the exact final prompt beside the image. Never overwrite existing outputs; use a new descriptive slug.
- Inspect once. Regenerate with one targeted correction if the image becomes a clean scrapbook template, loses the trash materials, becomes full-bleed scenery, lacks readable paper field, turns too cute, or makes text/brands too prominent.

## Hard Avoids

Always avoid:

- clean botanical scrapbook, polished stationery, wedding mood board, or influencer flat-lay
- retail craft kit, product catalog, packaging display, logo, CTA, price tag, or brand lockup
- full-bleed fantasy forest painting with no handmade paper construction
- digital UI, dashboard, app cards, website mockup, or social-media template
- glossy 3D render, cinematic scene, hard studio shadows, plastic-perfect surfaces
- cute kawaii stickers, anime, cartoon, children's classroom craft unless explicitly requested
- too many saturated colors, rainbow beads everywhere, or incoherent object dumping
- long readable paragraphs, copied newspaper text, usernames, watermarks, signatures, exact book pages
- realistic trash pile with dirtiness but no composition
- minimal poster so sparse that the art-trash material language disappears

## Output Format

Return the generated image, exact final prompt, and:

- Layout: selected family
- Materials: reclaimed objects and dominant trash texture
- Typography: title and label treatment
- one short interpretation note

## Quality Gate

Before finalizing, check:

- Is the result a tactile art-trash journal page rather than a clean scrapbook?
- Does aged paper or newspaper remain visibly present?
- Is there one controlled dense collage zone, not uncontrolled full-page clutter?
- Are low-value materials visible: beads, scraps, glue, moss, thread, flowers, labels, cardboard, or miniatures?
- Does the composition preserve enough negative space from Minimal Zine?
- Is one color anchor clear without becoming rainbow craft noise?
- Is type short, handmade, and subordinate?
- Are shadows shallow and physical, not glossy 3D?
- Does the image avoid brands, copied text, UI, retail craft styling, and polished fantasy scenery?
- Did you generate and inspect the final raster image?
