# Style Grammar

Use this file when reverse-engineering a reference, diagnosing style drift, or preserving the torn-paper relic system.

## Visual DNA

- Vertical poster, usually 3:4 or 4:5.
- Large off-white, cream, or light-gray paper field.
- One compact cluster of torn scraps, tape, stamp marks, and cut fragments.
- Single vivid accent block or mark, kept small but visible.
- Short Chinese or mixed Chinese/English text, printed or stamped.
- Matte scan look, paper fibers, dust, faint creases, and worn edges.

## Reference Extraction

Extract:

- paper tone and fiber texture
- cluster placement and size
- tear shape, overlap, and edge wear
- stamp or seal geometry
- accent color and its material form
- title placement, subtitle treatment, and text density
- whether the image feels like a note, envelope, sign, trace, or recovered fragment

## Remnant Rules

- Favor torn edges over clean cut rectangles.
- Use translucent tape, envelope flaps, paper folds, or residue from removed stickers.
- Let the scrap cluster feel recovered rather than arranged.
- Keep one accent shape stronger than the rest, but not huge.
- Let typography sit as a secondary physical mark, not a commercial title bar.

## Style Drift Corrections

If it becomes scrapbook-like:

- remove decorative stickers, ribbon, lace, Polaroid framing, and excessive layering
- simplify to one remnant cluster and one accent shape

If it becomes a postcard or ad:

- remove borders, CTA, logo feel, and glossy presentation
- strengthen the paper field and short printed text

If it becomes too plain:

- add one stamp circle, tape seam, torn flap, or color block
- keep the negative space intact

If the accent disappears:

- describe the accent as a brush block, arrow, seal, or tape strip
- make it the single chromatic anchor
