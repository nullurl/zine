# Style Grammar

## Core Identity

This is a quiet field notebook built from panoramic landscape windows. It combines the photographic sequence of a contact sheet with the incomplete evidence of a hand-kept journal: a pencil contour, an underlined word, a weather mark, a small map, or a translucent color test.

The page should feel assembled on matte paper, but it is not a maximal scrapbook. The repeated rounded strips create the hard structure; hand-drawn matter supplies irregular human attention within that structure.

## Stable Geometry

- Tall 3:5 or 2:3 page, white or warm off-white.
- Four to six equal horizontal windows, five by default.
- Identical large rounded corners and narrow even gutters.
- No shadows, no interface-card appearance, and no decorative outer border.
- Photo strips use panoramic crops and distant horizons.
- Drawing strips preserve the same width and radius even when their internal mark-making is loose.

## Cell Roles

### Photo Strip

One real landscape moment: cloud, tree line, power pole, moon, house, garden, field, road, water, or animal. Use natural exposure, analog softness, and one scale marker.

### Photo Plus Drawing

Keep the photograph readable. Add a single pencil outline, ink arrow, circle, plant contour, cloud edge, moon phase, or route line. The mark must point to or trace something present in the photograph.

### Hand-Drawn Study

Use graphite, colored pencil, wax crayon, dry brush, fountain pen, or translucent watercolor. Draw one observed subject, not a decorative scene. Preserve erasure, uneven pressure, white paper, and unfinished edges.

### Map or Notation

Use a sparse horizon diagram, weather symbol, compass fragment, route line, date, coordinate, or plant label. It may occupy a cell or gutter, but never becomes an infographic dashboard.

### Material Strip

Use tracing paper, rubbing, a small swatch, or a torn archive fragment only when it records a tactile memory. Keep it flat and subordinate to the panoramic rhythm.

## Minimal Zine Fusion

Inherited rules:

- clear attention geometry and active negative space
- one visual cluster with a restrained support system
- a single visible high-chroma anchor
- small serif, typewriter, or monospaced text
- old-paper fibers, scan noise, halftone, ink bleed, and slight registration drift
- matte, flat, orthographic paper view rather than glossy depth

Adapted rules:

- the visual cluster is distributed across 4-6 stable strips
- one imageable anchor exists per cell, but all anchors belong to one memory
- the high-chroma hue may be a drawn line, field patch, moon rim, flower, roof, or color swatch
- hand-drawn interventions are sparse evidence, not decoration

## Material and Color

Use a subdued natural family such as cloud cream and blue, blue-green rural dusk, charcoal moonlight, mist gray garden, or field green with warm earth. Keep color present in the photographs while muting secondary paper and text.

The accent hue must remain opaque and visible. A cobalt pencil contour, pear-green field mark, lemon weather symbol, or rust-orange roof detail can carry the accent. Do not apply a global saturated filter.

## Failure Diagnosis

### Looks like a UI grid

Remove shadows, card language, rounded button semantics, and perfect digital cleanliness. Add flat paper, film grain, visible gutters, imperfect pencil pressure, and hand-set spacing.

### Looks like a scrapbook

Remove stickers, tape, stamps, multiple captions, and unrelated scraps. Return to one mark per drawing cell and one anchor per photo cell.

### Looks like children's illustration

Use observational linework, restrained proportions, unfinished edges, documentary sky, real landscape scale, and no faces or mascot figures.

### Drawing floats without meaning

Trace or annotate a feature actually visible in the neighboring photo: cloud edge, tree silhouette, pole wire, roof line, moon contour, or route.

### Color anchor disappears

Name the hue and medium precisely, enlarge it to a readable mark or subject detail, and keep all competing colors neutral.
