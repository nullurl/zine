# Stationery Showcase Memo Zine

Turn the user's content into both:

1. a final image-generation prompt, and
2. a generated raster image made from that prompt.

## Source Grammar

Use this visual grammar as the style core:

- **Setting:** a quiet stationery shop, gallery display, or glass display case with pale gray shelves, powder-blue backing board, off-white wall, and soft indoor light.
- **Display system:** clipped paper samples, grid memo sheets, monthly calendar cards, small notebooks, ring-bound memo pads, packaged sticky notes, acrylic bins, wire stands, metal binder clips, and tiny product labels.
- **Paper language:** cream, white, pale gray, translucent vellum, graph paper, rounded-corner memo cards, thin calendar columns, tiny checklists, handwritten Korean or Chinese notes, arrows, circled marks, underlines, and micro captions.
- **Sticker language:** pastel sticky strips, circular dots, small black word labels, tiny icons, cutout stickers, colored tabs, square push pins, and occasional corrugated metal or plastic texture.
- **Color logic:** soft stationery palette: powder blue, mint, sage green, butter yellow, pale peach, lavender gray, warm cream, small navy/black text, with one or two tiny saturated marks such as red dot, blue arrow, or green label.
- **Camera:** real phone-photo realism with gentle glass glare, slight perspective, shelf edge, acrylic reflections, and tactile paper depth.
- **Mood:** analogue keeper, record book, daily letter, memo archive, quiet retail display, private planning ritual, stationery lab.

## Mode Policy

Use **Standard Mode** for all generation. Compile only the visual details that should become pixels. Do not include analysis prose, source filenames, or process notes inside the final image prompt.

## Standard Mode Prompt Compiler

Every prompt must describe a single finished still image, not a UI, mockup template, or clean digital poster.

### First-Principles Fields

1. **Canvas and Environment**
   - Vertical 3:5 phone-photo frame by default.
   - Pale gray shelf, soft off-white wall, powder-blue display backing, or glass case interior.
   - Include realistic display context: shelf lip, acrylic box, wire stand, glass reflection, wall texture, push pins, or clipped board.
   - Keep 35%-60% of the frame calm wall/shelf negative space, not a full-bleed collage.

2. **Primary Display Architecture**
   - Choose one main arrangement: pinned exhibition board, shelf product vignette, acrylic memo-bin display, ring-bound flip memo, standing clipboard calendar, or layered loose-paper stack.
   - Arrange 2-6 paper pieces with clear hierarchy.
   - Use rounded-corner memo cards, calendar strips, grid paper, sticky note samples, small product placards, or clipped sheets.

3. **Memo and Calendar System**
   - Convert the user's theme into a planning or archive page: monthly calendar, weekly timeline, checklist, index tab, focus-on card, diary note, or record book page.
   - Add tiny handwritten Korean or Chinese notes, short English labels, circled dates, arrows, underlines, check boxes, and micro grid structures.
   - Text should be visually credible and short; avoid long clean paragraphs.

4. **Sticker and Object Anchors**
   - Add 1-3 small sticker anchors based on the user's theme: simple cutout figure, tiny house, tree, cup, book, arrow, dot cluster, label strip, landscape rectangle, icon, or paper specimen.
   - Use tactile paper edges, white sticker outlines, halftone print, colored tabs, or translucent tape.
   - The anchor should feel like a stationery sample, not a polished illustration.

5. **Retail and Gallery Details**
   - Add small display labels such as `Record Book`, `Daily Letter`, `focus on`, `date`, `memo`, `Forest`, `Breath`, `Wind`, or a user-supplied short phrase.
   - Include product-display artifacts: metal clips, binder rings, acrylic pockets, plastic sleeves, stacked memo pads, price-card scale, push pins, or wire easels.
   - Use fictional or generic tiny branding only; do not reproduce real logos as the main subject.

6. **Color and Texture**
   - Keep the scene pastel and quiet, not high-saturation scrapbook.
   - Use powder blue or pale gray as the dominant backing color when appropriate.
   - Support it with mint, sage, cream, lavender, butter yellow, peach, and small black typography.
   - Allow 1-3 small stronger accents such as red dot, cobalt arrow, navy sticker, or green square, each thumbnail-visible but small.
   - Include paper fibers, plastic sleeve glare, acrylic reflection, soft shadows, slightly imperfect printing, and phone-photo grain.

7. **Emotional Temperature**
   - Quiet, curated, analogue, diaristic, gentle, collected, shop-display intimacy.
   - The viewer should feel they are looking into a small stationery exhibition, not a product ad.

8. **Hard Avoids**
   - Avoid clean vector UI, digital planner screenshot, ecommerce product listing, glossy studio product mockup, commercial headline poster, maximal scrapbook, kawaii overload, anime scene, fashion editorial, neon, 3D render, harsh cinematic lighting, perfectly readable long text, or fake floating objects with no shelf/display context.

### Standard Prompt Shape

Write the final Standard Mode prompt as four compact paragraphs:

1. environment, camera, shelf/display context, negative space
2. main paper arrangement, memo/calendar/page structure, handwritten annotation system
3. sticker anchors, product-display details, labels, clips, acrylic/plastic/ring elements
4. pastel color palette, tactile texture, mood, lighting, avoid-list

Prefer concrete object placement and material details over abstract adjectives. If the user supplies no display text, invent 2-4 short labels using a mix of English and Korean or Chinese.

## Workflow

1. Determine mode.
   - Use Standard Mode.

2. Parse the user's content.
   - Identify the core subject, mood, exact text if supplied, and any reference image role.
   - Convert abstract content into one planning/archive metaphor: calendar, record book, focus memo, sticker sample, swatch sheet, or display label.
   - If no text is supplied, invent short labels such as `Daily Letter`, `focus on`, `Record Book`, `memo`, `date`, or a 2-6 character Chinese/Korean-style headline.

3. Select a variation recipe.
   - Pick layout, memo structure, sticker anchor, display prop, color mode, text mode, and mood.
   - Keep the image recognizably a real stationery display. Add shelf, stand, glass, acrylic, clip, pin, or packaging context.
   - If the arrangement becomes too busy, reduce the number of paper pieces before removing tactile display details.

4. Write the final image prompt.
   - Use the four-paragraph Standard Prompt Shape.
   - Specify the camera angle, main display surface, paper count, prop placement, and dominant pastel palette.
   - Keep exact in-image text short. Image models distort long text; use microtext visually for dense handwriting.

5. Generate the image.
   - Use the built-in image generation capability by default.
   - Do not stop after prompt-only unless the user explicitly asks for prompt-only.
   - If the result becomes a clean digital planner, glossy ecommerce mockup, or chaotic scrapbook, tighten the real shelf/glass display, paper texture, pastel restraint, and handwritten memo language and regenerate once.

6. Return the image and prompt.

## Negative Constraints

Always avoid:

- clean digital planner screenshot or UI interface
- ecommerce product listing, isolated product on pure white, commercial ad layout, logo-first design
- glossy studio mockup, 3D render, CGI plastic, hard spotlight, dramatic depth of field
- maximal colorful scrapbook, washi tape overload, cute sticker flood, anime or kawaii poster style
- full-bleed illustrated scene instead of stationery display
- perfect vector text, long readable paragraphs, large typographic headline poster
- neon, cyberpunk, fashion editorial drama, luxury branding, busy unrelated collage
- copied real brand logos as the main visual anchor

## Quality Gate

Before finalizing, check:

- Did the prompt create a real stationery-shop, shelf, wall-board, or glass-case display?
- Is there visible display context such as acrylic, clips, pins, rings, shelf edges, stands, labels, or glass glare?
- Are the main objects paper-based memo cards, calendar sheets, sticky notes, notebooks, or sample swatches?
- Does the image include grid structures, dates, focus cards, checklists, arrows, circles, or tiny handwritten annotations?
- Does the palette stay soft: powder blue, pale gray, cream, mint, sage, lavender, peach, or butter yellow?
- Are any saturated marks small and purposeful rather than taking over the whole image?
- Does the composition retain calm negative space and curated hierarchy?
- Does it feel like analogue stationery archive or shop display rather than a digital poster?
- Are product labels fictional/generic and not copied as main logos?
- Did the prompt avoid UI, ecommerce, glossy mockup, 3D, neon, anime, and chaotic scrapbook aesthetics?
- Did you actually generate the image?
