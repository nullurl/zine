# Soft Focus Memory Album Style Grammar

## Background Field

- Use a real-feeling environment as a blurred full-frame field: tree-lined street, café interior, apartment courtyard, park, station, shoreline, or evening road.
- Defocus the field heavily enough that it reads as atmosphere, not as a competing photograph.
- Keep contrast low and color graded toward olive, warm gold, gray-green, muted cyan, or dusk blue.
- Let empty background zones breathe around the photo cluster. Do not turn the backdrop into a sharp location shot.

## Photo Cluster

- Use 5-9 photos from one memory thread.
- Place one anchor photo at 25%-42% of the cluster area.
- Add 4-8 smaller support crops with varied aspect ratios.
- Use thin white or warm-white borders on 40%-70% of the windows. Leave some crops borderless or translucent for rhythm.
- Overlap only enough to create a designed board; keep every important subject inspectable.
- Keep a shared time-of-day and grade. The source references use personal snapshots, not polished stock photography.

## Overlay Marks

Use only 2-4 overlay families per image:

- thin white outlined rectangles around a crop
- a fine connector line between two related windows
- one or two hand-drawn arrows, usually red or white
- small circles or crosshairs identifying a detail
- dot or star halftone clusters as soft texture
- a faint translucent ellipse or rounded overlay

Do not use every overlay family at once. Marks should guide reading like an edited album note.

## Type System

Primary title:

- handwritten brush script, loose cursive, or thin marker lettering
- 2-4 words, placed above or beside the cluster
- white, cream, or one muted accent color

Secondary type:

- small serif, mono, or narrow editorial sans
- short subline, date, mood tag, or location fragment
- low contrast and partially obscured where appropriate

Avoid long text that will turn into unreadable model-generated typography. Use only short exact phrases.

## Color Grade

The references favor a darkened green environment with warm café, skin, wood, and sunset highlights. Convert that grammar as needed:

- olive-black background
- warm tan and amber interior light
- muted green foliage
- faded cream type and borders
- optional vermilion arrow or cobalt marker

For brighter memories, lift midtones without removing the soft background blur. Keep the photo cluster readable and use one accent color as a design device.

## Difference From Minimal Zine

Minimal zine contributes focus, sparse text, one accent, and emotional restraint. This skill intentionally adds:

- landscape rather than vertical framing
- multiple photographic windows rather than one tiny anchor
- a blurred real-world field behind the cluster
- digital editorial lines and annotations rather than paper fibers as the main structure
- personal-memory sequencing rather than an isolated specimen

Do not bring over the original 70%-90% paper rule or force a physical paper surface unless requested.

## Drift Corrections

If the result becomes a generic moodboard:

- name one day/place/event and require every crop to belong to it
- make one anchor photo larger and establish a clear reading path
- remove unrelated objects and reduce decorative overlays

If the background competes:

- strengthen `heavily blurred, low-contrast, atmospheric background field`
- reduce backdrop detail and increase clear space around the cluster

If it becomes an advertisement:

- remove brand copy, CTA, logo, and product language
- strengthen `personal photo diary`, `casual snapshots`, and `quiet memory board`

If it becomes too busy:

- limit to 5-7 windows, one title, two captions, one arrow family, and one accent hue
