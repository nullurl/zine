# Archival Dossier Zine Style Grammar

Use this reference when reverse-engineering visual references, correcting style drift, or compiling a high-fidelity prompt.

## 1. Attention Geometry

- Use a seamless black or near-black field as the outer stage.
- Keep 50%-78% of the frame visually empty.
- Isolate one document object or one related object pair.
- Place a single object slightly off-center; place paired states on a clear vertical or horizontal axis.
- Keep the full silhouette visible. Do not crop hardware or page edges unless the user asks for a close-up.

## 2. Camera and Light

- Prefer overhead catalog view, flat archive scan, or restrained 10-25 degree perspective.
- Use diffuse neutral light with enough side definition to show thickness and paper curl.
- Keep shadows shallow and close to the object. The black stage should not become a dramatic spotlight scene.
- Use modest film softness, scan grain, or catalog-photography blur; avoid razor-sharp CGI edges.

## 3. Artifact Construction

Build the artifact from 3-7 related physical systems:

- kraft board, gray pressboard, ivory forms, tracing paper, onionskin, or card stock
- paper pockets, folded flaps, file dividers, edge tabs, or envelopes
- binder rings, split pins, paper clips, bulldog clips, staples, wire loops, thread, or prong fasteners
- punched holes, clipped corners, perforations, page notches, index tabs, and reinforced holes
- black-and-white evidence photos, contact prints, diagrams, specimen cards, or material samples

Hardware must have a functional reason. Do not scatter clips as decoration.

## 4. Information Architecture

Use institutional document logic:

- title block, accession code, date, place, subject, and page number
- ruled tables, check boxes, index columns, routing fields, measurement grids
- typed headings paired with handwritten notes
- faded rubber stamps, approval marks, classification-style labels, or filing dates
- one centered evidence image or two sequential photo windows
- one simple diagram that explains the photo or artifact

Primary hierarchy: 1-4 short exact labels.
Secondary hierarchy: semi-legible form text and annotations.
Tertiary hierarchy: registration marks, page codes, and faded marginal traces.

## 5. Typography

- Use typewriter mono, administrative grotesk, narrow serif, rubber stamp, graphite cursive, or technical lettering.
- Combine at most three typographic voices in one artifact.
- Allow skew, uneven ink density, misregistration, fading, underlining, and manual correction.
- Keep long body copy indistinct. Image models should not be asked to typeset full legal or historical documents.

## 6. Color Systems

Neutral base:

- ivory, cream, oxidized beige, kraft, warm gray, cool gray, graphite, black

Ink and hardware:

- black, brown-black, faded navy, cobalt pencil, oxidized steel, dull brass

Accent families:

- faded red stamps for authority and indexing
- saturated yellow for experiment cards or warning inserts
- cyan/blue for technical forms and modern editorial systems
- powder pink for translucent manual sheets
- orange or blue tabs for chronological filing

Use one accent family per output. Color should organize information, not decorate empty space.

## 7. Wear and Reproduction

Use 4-8 subtle defects:

- softened or rounded corners
- uneven page edges
- oxidation spots or rust transfer
- fold marks and pressure dents
- rubbed type and faded stamps
- graphite smears and pen corrections
- slight page warping or transparent-paper curl
- copy-machine haze, scan noise, or offset ghosting

Avoid uniformly dirty paper, coffee-stain clichés, burnt edges, and theatrical distressing.

## 8. Reference Analysis Checklist

For each supplied reference, record:

1. artifact state: closed, open, single sheet, ring-bound, or paired
2. silhouette and frame share
3. paper substrates and layer count
4. hardware and binding logic
5. information hierarchy
6. photo or diagram windows
7. accent color and its functional role
8. wear pattern and reproduction method
9. camera angle and shadow behavior
10. elements to abstract rather than copy

Compile the shared grammar across references. Do not reproduce source-specific names, document text, institutions, serial numbers, or signatures.

## 9. Drift Corrections

If the output looks like a stationery ad:

- strengthen `found institutional artifact`, `functional hardware`, `used paper`, and `catalog archive photograph`
- remove brand-language terms such as premium, elegant, luxury, or product mockup

If it looks like a flat poster:

- strengthen page thickness, open pocket, visible rings, curled tracing sheet, stacked edges, and clip pressure

If it looks like a scrapbook:

- reduce ephemera count, remove decorative tape, and organize every insert under one filing system

If it is too monochrome:

- enlarge one functional color insert, tab, stamp, or photo annotation while keeping the black stage dominant

If it is too colorful:

- return all support papers to ivory/kraft/gray and keep only one accent family
