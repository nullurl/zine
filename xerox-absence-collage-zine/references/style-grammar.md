# Reference-Derived Style Grammar

Use this document when analyzing supplied references, selecting structural measurements, or correcting a generation that has drifted toward generic scrapbook collage.

## 1. Stable Visual Identity

The reference set behaves like pages from an experimental literary photocopy archive. Nature photographs are not presented as documentary windows; they are treated as damaged evidence. Mountains, birds, flowers, water, trees, eyes, and an anonymous figure are repeated through cropping, inversion, color separation, blur, and generation loss.

Stable signals:

- vertical scanner-page presentation with visible white paper
- three to seven hard-edged rectangular image plates
- one dominant photo plate and several interrupted fragments
- mostly parallel edges with very little rotation
- rough overlap rather than clean modular spacing
- coarse photocopy grain across both image and paper
- short typewriter poems as material blocks
- forest/charcoal support with one cobalt or violet exposure event
- subjects associated with absence, forgetting, distance, trace, passage, and memory

## 2. Composition Measurements

### Dense exposure pages

- visible paper: 12%-25%
- main image plate: 45%-62%
- lower overlapping plate: 25%-42%
- narrow poem strip: 8%-15% page width
- dominant transformed subject: 18%-35%
- high-chroma cobalt bloom: 6%-14%

Typical map:

```text
paper margin
  narrow poem strip | large dark mountain/photo plate
                     | cobalt apparition
  texture scraps ----+----------------------------
       eye fragment  | lower dark negative plate
                     | white flower / void subject
```

### Mountain archive pages

- visible paper: 18%-32%
- main landscape plate: 45%-65%
- offset secondary landscape: 18%-32%
- portrait/object insert: 6%-12%
- typewriter patch: 6%-12%
- bottom terrain/water strip: 8%-16%

Typical map:

```text
  pale damaged background plate
    offset color plate + translucent poem patch
          large mountain/forest image
 torn seam + small portrait evidence
  ground fragment          river-stone strip
```

### Pale migration pages

- visible paper and fog: 50%-70%
- translucent sky rectangles: 24%-45%
- bird field: 12%-25% of page, distributed with air
- central poem patch: 8%-16%
- bottom field/mountain strip: 20%-32%
- color: near-monochrome except a faint mineral blue or green-gray plate

### Remains atlas pages

- visible paper: 45%-65%
- main haze/forest plate: 25%-42%
- five to eight smaller fragments: each 3%-12%
- type blocks: 10%-20% total, separated rather than one paragraph
- collage cluster stays off-center and may leave a large empty upper or right field

## 3. Layer Order

The physical logic matters more than the exact motif.

1. scanner white or worn fibrous paper
2. one pale background plate or fog wash
3. dominant landscape/image plate
4. one offset or overlapping image state
5. one transformed anchor: negative flower, cobalt body, birds, or portrait fragment
6. translucent poem patch or narrow type strip
7. one tape fold, torn seam, scratch, or registration scar

Do not place every fragment on an individual drop shadow. Depth comes from opacity changes, crop interruption, exposed paper fibers, and overlapping toner.

## 4. Subject Transformation Grammar

Use one pair per image:

- mountain: deep green positive + blue-gray offset copy
- flower: dim botanical photo + blown-out white negative silhouette
- body/hand: grayscale hidden figure + electric cobalt exposure bloom
- birds: sharp silhouettes + two or three motion-smeared echoes
- portrait: black silhouette + cold violet face field + white torn line across the eyes
- tree: black photocopy + inverted foliage window
- water: granular landscape + narrow high-contrast river strip
- eyes: cropped eye pair + simplified blue-gray mask field

Transformation should imply memory failure or material reproduction, not visual effects for their own sake.

## 5. Typography Grammar

- rough monospaced typewriter, small to medium size
- carbon-ribbon gaps, doubled impressions, irregular line spacing
- left-aligned narrow strip, centered short stanza, or separated evidence blocks
- lowercase English is common in the references, but new output may use exact Chinese or English user copy
- copy is poetic and declarative, not a caption describing the image
- no author or signature unless the user provides one
- no copied reference wording

For image generation, keep exact copy to one sentence or four short lines. Longer supplied text may be represented as a degraded visual block only when exact legibility is not required.

## 6. Color Grammar

Base:

- scanner white `#F2F2EE`
- fog gray `#C8CDCB`
- charcoal `#2E3330`
- forest black-green `#183128`
- mineral blue-gray `#708B9A`

Primary events:

- electric cobalt `#315CFF` to `#668BFF`
- ultraviolet blue `#5368D8`
- cold violet `#6870BF`

Rare residue:

- oxidized peach `#C49682`
- moss gray-green `#687A67`

The exact hex values guide relationships, not digital flat fills. Print them through uneven toner and paper texture.

### Palette Preservation Map

When a supplied reference controls color, preserve color by spatial role rather than averaging it into one grade.

| Role | Reference behavior | Typical share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| scanner white / paper fog | outer page, rubbed gaps, negative space | 20%-60% |
| charcoal / forest black | deepest photo plate, silhouette, type | 20%-48% |
| forest / moss green | ground, mountain, tree, riverbank | 10%-32% |
| mineral blue / blue-gray | cloud, atmosphere, offset duplicate | 10%-35% |
| cobalt / ultraviolet | exposed hand, flower, cloud scar, one plate | 3%-14% |
| cold violet | portrait field, twilight plate, eye mask | 2%-10% |
| oxidized peach | rubbed emulsion or soil residue only | 0%-2% |

Color-lock rules:

- Preserve the hue family and approximate region share before matching brightness.
- Keep forest green and mineral blue visible as separate plates; do not blend them into teal.
- Keep cobalt saturated at the white-hot center of an exposure bloom.
- Keep violet attached to a portrait, eye strip, or twilight field rather than applying purple globally.
- Keep the paper neutral; it must not inherit the cobalt or green grade.
- Preserve the reference's color noise, uneven toner, and faded edges without lowering every colored region to gray.

### Extracted Element Inventory

These are reusable structural elements visible across the reference set. Select two to five per page.

- mist mountain with layered receding ridges
- blue-gray cloud drifting across a green-black mountain
- forest/ground block with visible rectangular color separation
- river stones, water glare, meadow, or distant terrain as a narrow bottom anchor
- pale branch canopy or rubbed foliage used as a background transparency
- cobalt hand/body/flower apparition with blown white center
- clipped white flower negative over a dark green plate
- narrow eye pair in a blue-gray mask rectangle
- cold-violet anonymous portrait with a black torso silhouette
- torn white fiber or scratch masking the portrait's eyes
- mixed sharp and blurred birds distributed across overlapping translucent sky plates
- top-edge tape fold with creases, trapped air, lifted corner, and milky reflection
- left or bottom xerox abrasion strip with reversed black/white texture
- fragmented typewriter poem split into one primary block and one or two isolated lines

Pairing guidance:

- mountain + portrait + river strip
- apparition + eye strip + flower negative
- bird flock + field strip + centered poem patch
- forest haze + white flower + separated type blocks
- cloud plate + rubbed branch transparency + terrain fragment

Never combine portrait, eye strip, apparition, flower negative, and bird flock all at once. Richness comes from layer interaction, not motif count.

### Occlusion Signatures

The references repeatedly use four kinds of interruption:

- a colored landscape plate cuts across a pale duplicate
- a portrait or eye strip interrupts a larger natural image
- a torn white fiber crosses the edge or eye line of an insert
- a poem patch overlaps atmosphere but avoids the darkest subject

Require two signatures per dense page and one per pale page.

## 7. Material Grammar

- xerox noise is coarse and nonuniform
- blue toner blooms toward white at the brightest center
- black regions contain green or graphite contamination
- transparent tape is milky and creased, not glossy stationery tape
- torn edges expose soft fibers
- image plates show generation loss, vertical scan bands, and rubbed emulsion
- rectangular seams remain visible and sometimes misregister by a few pixels
- white negative subjects clip aggressively and lose internal detail

## 8. Minimal Zine Fusion

Keep:

- one dominant attention event
- deliberate paper space
- one saturated hue family
- small typewriter typography
- flat scanned materiality
- poetic distance and anti-commercial tone

Expand:

- isolated small cluster -> overlapping image field
- tiny color object -> cobalt exposure subject or image plate
- sparse microtext -> short literary evidence block
- uniform blank paper -> alternating blank paper and dark copied plates

## 9. Reference Exclusions

The following are not transferable style tokens:

- exact poems or line breaks
- exact anonymous faces, eyes, or body poses
- artist names, signatures, watermarks, dates, or publication marks
- exact number and placement of birds
- exact flower species or mountain profile unless supplied as content
- screenshot borders, display scaling, or compression artifacts unrelated to the page

## 10. Failure Diagnostics

- **Looks like a scrapbook:** remove stickers, decorative tape, rotation, captions, and equal-size cards; restore hard rectangular photo plates and one overlap hierarchy.
- **Looks like a photo grid:** break the grid, enlarge one plate, overlap two edges, expose more irregular paper, and add one transformed subject.
- **Looks like a poster template:** shrink title, replace clean sans type with rough typewriter copy, remove centered branding hierarchy.
- **Too dark:** increase scanner white and fog plates; preserve one deep charcoal plate rather than lifting every black.
- **Too pale:** enlarge one dark plate and strengthen the cobalt/negative subject.
- **Color is decorative:** attach cobalt to the transformed subject or one complete image plate.
- **Reference color collapsed:** restore palette roles explicitly: green ground, mineral-blue atmosphere, cobalt exposure, violet insert, neutral paper; remove global desaturation and global tint.
- **Too many extracted motifs:** keep one subject family, one transformed echo, and at most three supporting traces; remove the least structurally connected insert.
- **Elements float like stickers:** make each insert cross a plate edge, become partially occluded, or share toner with the underlying image.
- **Texture is uniform:** specify toner clumps, edge rubbing, scan bands, and generation loss in separate regions.
